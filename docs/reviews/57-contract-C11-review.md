## Verdict

**REJECT.** 1 Blocker, 12 Majors, 7 Minors. All three Blockers of review 41 are resolved
as designed: each item carries its own context, NetFlow v9 has a packet-level identity, and
`provisioning.domains.v1` has been dropped in favour of observation-point facts with a
persisted, positioned and heartbeated feed. Every other review 41 finding is applied,
except F41-07, which is only partly applied (F57-05).

The new Blocker comes from the amended S-1. Coverage by heartbeat now gates only parts
that have **no** binding. A part that **does** match is bound at whatever binding position
stage A has reached. If stage A lags on the binding feed, an ended or reassigned binding
still looks live, and traffic is silently charged to the **previous tenant**. No check
catches this, and nothing corrects it afterwards. The Majors cluster on the seams of the
new fixes: what goes into `context` and how it is selected and logged, correction keys that
change on a resend, a correction deadline the exporter can move, a format switch that mixes
packet and record sequences, and the unresolved T4 hold.

| F57-02 | Major (money) | Part I "Identity and decoding come from the item alone"; RFC-003 §3.2 Epoch ("within `epoch_jitter` of **a** known epoch") | **The epoch a message maps to can change between the first send and a resend.** For NetFlow v9 the init time is `unix_secs × 1000 − sysUpTime`. `unix_secs` is NTP-steered, `sysUpTime` is free-running, so the derived value drifts (20 to 50 ppm gives 1.7 to 4.3 s a day, est.). IPFIX exporters that recompute IE 160 drift the same way. The partition therefore opens epochs whose init times are 2 to 4 s apart, and a message near the boundary is within 2 s of **two** epochs. The contract says neither which one it takes nor that the choice is stable. With a "nearest epoch" implementation: M (init time +1.9 s from E0's) is accepted in E0. Later E1 (+2.1 s) opens. M's resend is nearer E1, so it gets a new identity and is **accepted again**, charging twice. That is about 10⁸ epoch sets, more than 3.2 GB of range-set memory at 32 B per set minimum, about 25 MB per slot, against RFC-003 §3.5's 640 MB and 5 MB | Normative rule: a message takes the **lowest-numbered** epoch whose init time is within `epoch_jitter` of its own. Add a test: drift opens E1, then a resend of an E0 message stays a duplicate. Re-estimate RFC-003 §3.5 for drift, or anchor v9 epochs by sequence continuity (a new epoch only when the sequence also restarts) (AI-57-2) |

**Arithmetic rechecked.** Heartbeats 10⁴ / 5 s = 2,000/s ✓ (est.). Acknowledgement
5 + 10 + 30 + 30 + 10 = 85 ms ✓. `keep_by` 500 − 100 = 400 ms ✓. Exporter buffer: a hot
exporter at 50,000 records/s × 100 B = 5 MB/s, so 18 GB for 1 h, plus context ✓ (est.;
feasible on a relay's disk). Credit window values (`windowBatches`, `windowBytes`) and edge
memory per region (10⁴ sessions × window) are not given: an unmarked gap, folded into
F57-11. New estimates: epoch churn (F57-02), context overhead (F57-08), message memo
(F57-09), all marked (est.) above.

**Settled decisions applied consistently.** S-1 as amended matches Part III, test 18 and
the reject table. S-2 matches `IdentityConflictBody`, the Part II apply rule and test 20.
The amended S-1 makes the bound path's coverage gap (F57-01) visible, but does not cause it.

**Over-engineering.** None found. The v9 exception set is the cheapest correct option: a
record-level integer `packetExtSeq × 2¹⁶ + i` would leave one gap per packet and inflate
the range sets.

**NABC.** Not applicable (contract). Competition and benefits are RFC-003's.

## Decisions taken

| ID | Decision | Balance |
|---|---|---|
| D57-1 | Binding of **every** part waits for heartbeat coverage of its time. Provisioning may assert coverage up to `binding_lead` (60 s, est.) ahead, publishing each change at least that long before it takes effect | Buyers and AI agents are never charged for another tenant's traffic. Suppliers lose nothing (a delayed part is still charged). Share keeps the 0.3 s attribution path in steady state. Provisioning takes on a 60 s lead obligation, a small extension of R3-4 |
| D57-2 | Both interfaces present with no direction stays a rejection. Relays add `flowDirection` | Guessing "ingress" risks charging a wrong binding. A rejection is visible, correctable and not charged |
| D57-3 | The correction deadline is the rejected record's own last minute end (or receipt) + `dispute_window`. Correction times may not lie after the original's receipt + `future_skew_max` | The horizon is fixed for buyers, as D41-1 intended. Suppliers keep 30 days. Charges cannot be moved into minutes where the traffic never flowed |
| D57-4 | A T4 violation counts the affected parts in exposure but does not charge them. On resolution, the retracted binding's parts are dropped and the survivor's charged. Unresolved within `dispute_window`, they are dropped and finance is alerted | Share's credit risk stays bounded while provisioning repairs. Buyers are never double-charged. Suppliers are paid once the configuration is fixed |
| D57-5 | The export format is fixed per exporter ID. A switch is a new exporter ID, issued through C08 | It costs one credential and one provisioning change per switch, which is rare. It removes a revenue-loss and quarantine-flood mode |
| D57-6 | Corrections are deduplicated by a stable reject key derived from the item, not by `RawRef` | Suppliers can safely correct every rejection they are told of. Buyers are charged once (P3-S30) |
