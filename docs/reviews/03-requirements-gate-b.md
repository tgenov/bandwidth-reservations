## Verdict

**FAIL, with one Blocker and a bounded fix list.** Round 3/4 is a real repair. 39 of the
41 GB findings are closed in the specs. Every Examples row and worked example in P3, P4
and X2 (and the money rows of P2) was recomputed and is correct. That covers accruals,
LIFO decreases, spreading by seconds, per-line rounding, amendments, Webster with leg-order
ties and Share losing, the supplier-error split on R1, holds (future, fee, changes,
suspension), headroom with credit holds and usage exposure, the lateness bound, the resume
and warning amounts, cumulative key spending, the FX rounding both ways, payout residues
and netting. Traceability is mechanically clean: every need is cited, every tag names a
need, there are no duplicate IDs, and the conformance matrix lists every ID. Every
Outline's placeholders resolve.

One defect makes the baseline contradict itself. The reconciliation invariant that round 4
wrote into POL-24, P4-N14 and P4-S54 counts only **positive** buyer balances. The baseline
itself creates negative balances: a reversal of money already spent (P4-S70, −4.700000
USD) and every credit-limit account with posted charges (P4-S14). So the invariant is
wrong by exactly the sum of the negative balances, and a conforming system raises a false
alert every day those balances exist (GB2-01). The fix is one rule, decided below. The
eight Majors are mostly stale glossary definitions that contradict the round-4 numbers,
plus four undecided money edges and two X2 targets that cannot be measured as written.
All of them are decided below. After the fixes, re-gate only P4-N14 and S54, the glossary,
and the scenarios each fix names.

## Decisions taken

| # | Question | Decision | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | What exactly must the daily reconciliation prove? (GB2-01) | Funds at the partner = Σ buyer balances, **signed** (a negative balance is a receivable Share has funded) + Σ supplier balances, signed (net of supplier debts) + Share's own balance − payouts the partner has executed and the ledger has not yet confirmed (each matched to its partner debit). Safeguarding rule: Share's own balance must always cover the receivables it funds (negative buyer balances and supplier debts), so the funds at the partner always cover every positive buyer and supplier balance | Every micro is accounted for and safeguarded money never funds another buyer's debt. Share must pre-fund chargebacks and up to two months of credit-account charges |
| D2 | What payment does an insufficient-funds rejection quote? (GB2-04) | Two amounts: the minimum to be confirmed (hold − headroom), and that minimum plus `low_balance_warning`, the amount that also avoids an immediate low-balance state | An agent gets the exact minimum it can act on, plus the amount that does not lead to suspension a minute later. One more field to show |
| D3 | When does an account suspended for an overdue invoice resume? (GB2-05) | When every overdue invoice is paid **and** the POL-16 headroom rule holds. Headroom alone never resumes it | Credit terms are enforced. An enterprise that pays late loses its reservations after the grace, which is the policy's own consequence |
| D4 | What follows a write-off? (GB2-06) | For both reversals and invoices: the loss is booked to Share, but the written-off amount stays recorded as owed. The account stays suspended (no bookings) until a top-up repays it, and that repayment goes to Share's own account. After an invoice write-off the account also loses its credit limit and books prepaid until an operator re-vets it | Protects Share against chargeback and non-payment abuse. A returning buyer must settle first, which is fair because the debt was its own |
| D5 | How is a "duplicate payout" debt recovered? (GB2-07) | Like any supplier debt: netted against the next payouts and invoiced if open more than 90 days. It is shown on the statement as "duplicate payout", never as an earnings reduction, and it does not count in the deviation report | It reuses the POL-22 debt path. The supplier's earnings history stays clean, because the error was a transfer, not its own |
| D6 | What operation and latency does each availability probe use? (GB2-08) | Admission: a single-link booking answer, 200 ms. Usage ingestion: a record acknowledged as accepted, 1 s. Wallet: a balance/headroom answer, 200 ms (it gates admission). Payouts: a payout-status answer, 1 s. Reporting and statements: one day's statement, 2 s. Each is a per-probe limit | Every capability can be measured. The wallet answer is held to the booking budget because a booking depends on it |
| D7 | How do billing recovery and charge timeliness fit together? (GB2-09) | Ingestion is its own capability, with a 1-minute RTO like admission. "Rating and posting" has a 5-minute RTO and no availability target of its own; it is judged by timeliness. Charge timeliness is met when at least 99.99% of a month's minutes have their charges posted within target, and every charge is posted within the RTO plus the target | Removes the conflict without making rating as costly as admission. Buyers see at worst a 7-minute posting delay after a zone loss |
| D8 | Who pays a partner fee when a holder whose rate was cut under POL-07 cancels? (GB2-10) | Share, as for a revocation (POL-07, POL-23) | The cut was Share's decision. The buyer's free exit stays free and the partner is still paid |
| D9 | How does a fee hold behave over time and at release? (GB2-11) | The hold is max(coverage, fee) only while the fee could still be charged; after the minimum term it is the coverage. At release, the fee is charged against what is left of the hold, and any shortfall becomes a negative balance under POL-21 and POL-23 | Buyers are never held twice for partner routes. Share's loss is bounded by min(fee, coverage) per reservation |
| D10 | What does a buyer pay on a cheaper equivalent path? (GB2-12) | The lower of the original price and the new path's price. The new path's parties are split on what is charged. Share pays only when the new path is dearer | Share never profits from a supplier's exit, which supports carrier neutrality |
| D11 | How is a Share-borne usage-priced payment split? (GB2-13) | As the Webster split, among the route's parties, of the charge the traffic would have made. Each supplier is paid its share from Share's own account; the commission share is not posted | Suppliers are paid as if the buyer had paid, and there are no circular movements |
| D12 | How is a supplier with several legs on one route split? (GB2-14) | It is one party, with its legs' weights summed, and its tie position is that of its first leg | Deterministic, and it cannot game the split by listing a link as two legs |
| D13 | What does a negative key headroom do? (GB2-15) | It blocks new bookings and additions by that key only. It never ends, suspends or reduces reservations, and the account's other keys are unaffected | The limit stays a hard gate on new commitments without revoking guarantees the operator already approved |
| D14 | Is a cancellation fee a charge line? (GB2-16) | Yes, a third kind: one fee for one reservation. Its amount is the exact shown amount in micros (no rounding), it is not split, and it is posted once by its source reference | It reuses the charge-line machinery and keeps POL-08 r4's "wholly to the partner" |
| D15 | Who may set credit limits and resolve money escalations? (GB2-18) | An operator in the finance role, with a reason, recorded as in X1-S12. Setting a credit limit also requires KYB (P4-S82) | One control model for every operator action that moves or unlocks money |
| D16 | What happens if a grown hold on a price change cannot be covered? (GB2-24) | The price still takes effect. The account is suspended as for a failed roll-forward, after the usual low-balance warning (the change was announced `price_notice` ahead) | The price lock rules are unchanged. The buyer had 30 days' notice and a warning |
