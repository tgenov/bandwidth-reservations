## Verdict

**ACCEPT WITH CHANGES. RFC-001, RFC-002 and RFC-004 must not be approved until A0-01 and A0-02 are resolved.**

The overall shape is right, and it is the cheapest one that can meet the requirements:

- one writer per partition, on a consensus log fenced by term;
- five voters placed 2+2+1 across three regions, so every majority spans two regions;
- facts kept append-only;
- deadlines treated as data;
- no global sequencer.

Two parts of the design fail as written:

- **The commit fence (A0-01) is unsafe.** It compares a decision's timestamp against a strong read by the link owner. A decision entry can commit after that read has returned "no decision". When that happens, a link releases capacity under a confirmed reservation, and a status read reports a failure that is later contradicted.
- **The account split (A0-02) breaks the requirements.** The account's admission state is split between two components that share the same partition key: the coordinator's request log, durable in two regions, and account funds, durable in one region. That split gives each request two decision points, so P2-S69 and P2-S103 cannot both hold. It also gives the account two serial orders, and the shown headroom (P4-S72) cannot be rebuilt after a region loss.

Both fixes stay inside the architecture. A0-01 needs a different expiry rule. A0-02 merges two components, which makes the design smaller. Both fixes are described below. Nine Major findings follow, most of them gaps the detailed RFCs would otherwise inherit.

**Counts:** 2 Blocker, 9 Major, 10 Minor. 7 decisions taken. 4 requirements issues.

## Explicit assumptions used for the arithmetic

The RFC says only that R1 and R2 are within 20 ms of each other. It does not say where any region is, or how far R3 is. The figures below are **estimates**, to be replaced by measurement (RFC-000 R1/R2).

| Pair | Assumed RTT (estimate) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| R1 Johannesburg ↔ R2 Cape Town | ~20 ms | the only plausible pair of separate cloud regions within 20 ms anywhere near the markets served |
| R1 or R2 ↔ R3 Nairobi (or the nearest East African region) | 50 to 80 ms | terrestrial and submarine paths |
| Any of them ↔ Marseille | 110 to 170 ms | |
| Between zones in one region | 1 to 2 ms | |
| Buyer or probe in Nairobi or Mombasa ↔ R1 | 50 to 70 ms | |

**Quorum latency with 2+2+1 voters** (5 voters, majority 3):

- **Steady state, leader in R1.** A commit needs the leader, its R1 peer, and the faster of the two R2 voters. That is one R1↔R2 round trip plus an fsync, about 22 to 25 ms at the median. **35 ms at p99 is credible** under group commit. R3 is never on the commit path.
- **After a zone loss in R1.** A commit needs the leader plus two of R2a, R2b and R3. That is still one R1↔R2 round trip. The estimate is unchanged.
- **After a region loss of R1.** The remaining voters are R2a, R2b and R3, three of five, so **every commit needs R3**. One commit is about 60 to 90 ms at p99. Any rule that keeps facts durable in two regions has this property: once one region is lost, every write must reach the far region. With two such writes in sequence, plus hops, a single-link booking exceeds 200 ms. See A0-05 and RQ-3.

**The RFC's ~100 ms p99 budget holds only if every one of these is true** (A0-05):

- the coordinator's code runs in the same region as every leader it calls;
- the account partition's writes really are 5 ms writes inside one region, which A0-02 shows is not sound;
- the measurement starts at the edge.

Two cases break it:

- **A coordinator worker in R2 calling leaders in R1.** Four sequential calls each add a 20 ms round trip, so the total is 10 + (20+5) + (20+35) + (20+5) + (20+35) + 10 ≈ **180 ms**.
- **A probe from Nairobi on a fresh TLS connection.** TCP, TLS 1.3 and the request are three round trips of about 60 ms each, about 180 ms before the server does anything.

**The design proposed in A0-02** writes, in order:

1. the request record and the funds hold, as one two-region entry (35 ms);
2. the link hold (35 ms);
3. the decision, which binds the funds, as one two-region entry (35 ms).

With the edge (10 ms) and hops between components in one region (10 ms), the total is about **125 ms at p99** (estimate). The RFC's "all two-region" fallback is about 170 ms; this is cheaper because it is one write fewer.

## Findings

| ID | Severity | Location | Finding | Proposed change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A0-01 | **Blocker** | §4.3 (commit fence); §3 deadline path; R4, R8 | **Safety depends on commit latency, which nothing bounds.** The decision is "written only if its commit timestamp is earlier than D − τ". The link owner releases at D − ε "after a strong read of the decision log shows no decision". The timestamp is assigned at proposal, but the entry *commits* when a majority stores it, which can be any time later: a GC pause, a slow follower, or a new leader committing an earlier term's entry. A linearizable read (ReadIndex) at D − ε does not see an entry that is proposed but not yet committed. So the read returns "no decision", the link releases the hold, and the decision then commits **confirmed**. The result is a confirmed route whose leg capacity was handed to a waiter: an oversell (P1-N1) or a route partly booked (P2-N1). The same race makes a status read report "failed as of D" and later contradict it (P2-S10, P2-S99). τ = 2ε + one read covers clock uncertainty only, not replication delay. | Make expiry a **log write, not a read**. At D − ε, each participant (or any party) appends a conditional "expire if undecided" entry to the request's decision log. The state machine keeps whichever of *confirmed* or *expired* comes first in log order. A participant releases its hold only once its expiry entry, or a later expired outcome, is committed. Clocks then drive liveness only. The rule "do not start committing after D − τ" stays, as a liveness optimisation. A status read reports "failed" only after an expired outcome is committed, or from a strong read made after the expiry write. The residual case, a decision log without quorum at D, keeps the hold (safe) and alerts. That needs RQ-1. |
| A0-02 | **Blocker** | §2 (booking coordinator / account funds split); §4.1 "account-level admission"; §5.3 row 4; §3 latency table; RI-3; §5.1 failure table | **Two decision points and two serial orders for one account.** There are four problems. **(a) POL-04 "one decision per request" is broken.** Suspension and key status are judged at the bind (account funds). Withdrawal and the deadline are judged at the decision record (coordinator). P2-S69 and P2-S103 cannot both hold (see the traces). The RFC's RI-3 reading was overruled by the amended POL-04. **(b) Durability is mixed inside one partition.** Entries committed inside one region cannot live in the 5-voter group: a leader elected in R2 without them loses them on an ordinary node failure, not only on a region loss. So account funds must be a separate 3-voter group in R1, while standing, credit limits and invoices are durable in two regions (§5.3). "One serial order per account covers all three" (§2) is then false: the bind reads standing from another log. **(c) Region loss destroys shown state.** Every hold, bind, key-spending counter and in-flight counter is lost with R1. Some amounts depend on history and cannot be derived from confirmed reservations: the hold grown "by whatever headroom remains" at a price-change suspension (POL-16, POL-27), a fee charged at release, roll-forward paused while suspended, and the once-per-crossing warning state. P4-S72 ("rebuilt … equal what the account was shown") then fails for every figure shown in the lost interval. **(d) The RFC contradicts itself on recovery.** §5.1 says that on loss of R1 "leaders move to R2 automatically … RTO well inside 1 h". But §5.3 needs every account partition rebuilt before admission can answer. | **Merge the coordinator's request log and account funds into one account state machine on a two-region (2+2+1) log**, partitioned by account. Reservations as route-level units can stay in it or in their own reservation partitions. Its entries are: (1) request intake (idempotency key, fingerprint, principal, D, age) and the funds or credit hold, as one entry; (2) progress, waiting and withdrawal marks; (3) the **decision**. The decision entry is the single POL-04 point: it checks standing, key status (A0-12), the withdrawal mark, any expiry entry (A0-01) and limits, and binds the funds, all in one serial order. Make RFC-004 Part A and RFC-002 joint owners of this one state machine, with one writer process. Estimated cost: three two-region writes per single-link booking, about 125 ms at p99, against about 100 ms now (the 100 ms rests on the unsound one-region writes). Delete §5.3 row 4, R6 and the "rebuild" fallback. |
| A0-03 | Major | §3 "Single-link booking … pending record is written only if a final answer is not ready"; §4.4 booking-request row | **No durable request record before the holds on the fast path (80% of load).** (a) Two concurrent submissions with the same key but different parameters reach the funds owner and the link owner in different orders. Each participant then refuses a different one, so both can fail. This contradicts "never changes the request that first used the key" (P2-S26, P2-S64, glossary *Not accepted*). (b) D and `request_age` are fixed "when received" but stored nowhere, so a retry after a crash gets a new deadline and a new priority. (c) POL-03 asks for automatic resumption, and P2-S23 and P2-S27 ask for a status after restart. Without a record there is nothing to resume or report. (d) After a region loss, capacity holds survive with no funds hold and no request (POL-25). | Write the request record first, atomically with the idempotency check, in the same entry as the funds hold (A0-02, entry 1). Each link hold carries the account, the request identity, D and age, so participants can be reconciled. |
| A0-04 | Major | §3 "partner refuses definitively"; §2 partner adapters; §4.5 "waiters on one link" | **The priority rule and the crossing rule are missing for Share's own positions at a partner.** The design gives no owner the job of ordering partner-refusal waiters or of making a younger waiting holder yield at a partner. ## Decisions taken

| # | Decision | Balance |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Geography.** R1 and R2 are the two separate South African cloud regions about 20 ms apart. R3 is the nearest East African region (Nairobi, est. 50 to 80 ms). A third region in the same metro as R1 or R2 is not allowed, because one disaster in that metro could lose a fact committed only in those two regions. Edge points of presence in Nairobi and Mombasa terminate TLS close to buyers. | RPO 0 is real, and steady-state latency is not affected. The cost is slower writes during a declared disaster, which RQ-3 covers. |
| D2 | **Probes** run from locations outside the cloud region but within about 10 ms of it (metro colocation), over persistent TLS connections. The 200 ms limit is measured end to end. | Measures the service honestly, without charging the internet path from far away to the availability budget. Buyers in Kenya are served by the edge points of presence (D1). |
| D3 | **The probe account** is a Share-internal account with a credit limit set by finance. It is never invoiced, and its holds are real but excluded (POL-06). | The real funds path is exercised with no money movements. |
| D4 | **An equivalent-path offer is firm.** Its capacity is held on the new path for `offer_window`, as a hold owned by the reservation's move command (RFC-001 and RFC-002), with no funds hold, because the price is no higher than the original. | An offer an agent can act on, at the cost of up to 7 days of unsold capacity on the new path, a rare event triggered by a supplier's exit. RQ-4 asks the requirements to say so. |
| D5 | **Rate limits** are enforced exactly at the account state machine. Edge counters are advisory. | Hard limits for agent operators, at one extra in-memory check per request. |
| D6 | **Key revocation** takes effect for pending requests when it is applied in the account state machine, at most 60 s after the administrator's action. | Gives P2-S72 and P2-S103 one order. |
| D7 | **Partner confirm** is issued only after the account state machine's "committing" entry (A0-09). | Minimises cancellations at Share's cost. |

## Requirements issues

| # | Requirement | Issue | Proposed amendment |
|---|---|---|---|
| RQ-1 | POL-26 ("holds must expire on their own"); P2-N3 measure "after its deadline on any leg … even across an outage of Share's booking process"; P2-S101 for Share links | An all-or-nothing commit across independent participants cannot also let each participant release at D on its own, unless the commit point itself is known to be final by D. Under realistic timing (no bound on message delay), that is true only while the request's decision log is available (A0-01). If that log has no quorum at D, a hold released at D could be followed by a confirmed decision. Keeping the hold is safe for overselling but exceeds D. | Add to POL-26: "On a Share link, capacity held for a request is released at its deadline. If Share's record of the request's decision is unavailable at the deadline, it is released within admission's node-or-zone RTO (1 min) after the deadline. | RQ-2 | P2-N3 measure; P2-N7; POL-28; POL-08 (who pays) | Share may confirm only legs the partner has confirmed, so a partner with holds is confirmed before Share's decision.
