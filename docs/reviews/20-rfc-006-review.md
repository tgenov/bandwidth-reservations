## Verdict

**REJECT: revise to revision 4.** The verdict counts 3 Blockers, 12 Majors and 17 Minors (32 findings).

Most of the RFC is strong: the zone configurations, the joint-configuration argument for the
automatic 2 + 2 change, the outbox with two publish paths, the alarm ladder, and the
probe-evidence declaration (X2-S29) are correct and carefully argued. Every X2 scenario is
traced. The rejection is about the **owner runtime (§4)**, the contract that RFC-001 to
RFC-005 all build on. In three places it does not deliver what RFC-000 §4.6 and §5.2 promise:

1. A session renewal can commit **after** a successor has acquired one of its slots. The old
   holder then believes it still holds the lease (B1).
2. Read leases can outlive the owner lease, and on a handoff the successor reports at once.
   Either way a reader can answer `pending` after the outcome was reported (B2).
3. `owner_session` and `owner_slot` sit in single ranges. Losing one database node can expire
   every session in the platform, and then every process exits and restarts cold (B3).

All three can be fixed locally and cheaply. The fixes are stated below. They have to be in the
TLA+ model before any owner RFC is approved against §12.

| R6-01 | **Blocker** | §4.2 renewal and acquisition; §12.2 `leaseValid()`; RFC-000 §5.2 | **A renewal can be serialised after a successor's acquisition.** The renewal is `UPDATE owner_session SET expires_at = now() + '3s' WHERE … AND expires_at > now()`. On CockroachDB, `now()` is the transaction's *start* timestamp `r`. Trace: the session's expiry `E` falls between `r` and the standby's acquisition at `a > E`. The acquisition reads the session row at `a` (expired) and commits the slot to itself. It writes nothing to `owner_session`. The renewal's write meets the acquisition's read in the timestamp cache, so it is pushed above `a`. Its read refresh then succeeds, because nobody wrote the session row between `r` and `a`. The renewal commits with `expires_at = r + 3 s`, which is later than `a`. The old holder's renewal "succeeded", so its own view runs to `t_send + 3 s − ε`, while the slot row names the successor. For up to about 3 s the deposed process passes `leaseValid()`: it grants and renews read leases, answers headroom from memory, and sends commands from memory. Its commits fail the epoch check, but its leased readers answer rule 2 `pending` for requests the successor has already decided and reported. That breaks "never `pending` after a report" (P2-S10, P2-S108, RQ-13) and RFC-002 §8.2's P2-S111 argument. The renewal can be in flight across the expiry whenever renewals were delayed by 2.5 s (a GC pause, a slow gateway, a range-lease move). RFC-000 §5.2's "renewals never conflict" is the root cause | Make every acquisition **conflict** with the old session's renewals. In the acquisition transaction, add `UPDATE owner_session SET revoked = true WHERE session_id = <old>`, and have the renewal require `NOT revoked`. Acquisitions are rare, so this costs nothing measurable. A renewal that meets a revocation fails, and the process gives up its session (§4.4). An alternative is for the renewal to stamp and check against `cluster_logical_timestamp()`, which forbids the push. Add this interleaving to the TLA+ model and the Jepsen harness (§22.2), and add a DT ("a renewal in flight across expiry never extends a session whose slot was taken"). Raise the matching RFC-000 amendment (AI-R6-1) |
| R6-02 | **Blocker** | §4.6 read leases; §4.2 handoff; §4.4 "Takeover entry and resumption"; DT-05; RFC-000 §4.6 | **Read leases can outlive the owner lease that granted them, and handoff skips the wait altogether.** (a) §4.6 says only that the holder grants "within its own owner lease". It never requires `now + read_lease ≤ untilMonotonic`. A lease granted 1 ms before the holder's view ends is honoured by the reader for about 300 ms more, past the database expiry. A successor that acquires at expiry and reports at once (§4.4: "which they have, since the old session expired first") reports while the old reader still answers rule 2 `pending`. (b) **Handoff** (§4.2: "the successor starts at once, without waiting for an expiry") is used for every rebalance, `drift_correct`, offset hand-off and rolling deploy (RB-13), so thousands of slots a day move this way. The old holder's read leases are still live, and the successor knows nothing of them. RFC-000 §4.6's safety argument ("a new owner … can take the owner lease only then") does not hold for handoff. The result is `pending` after a report, so P2-S10, P2-S108 and P2-S111 fail at every deploy | (1) Grant or renew a read lease only if `now_mono + read_lease ≤ untilMonotonic` of the holder's own lease. (2) On handoff, the old holder stops granting at the handoff transaction. The successor neither reports nor advances the report position until `read_lease + ε` (500 ms) after its acquisition commit. Equivalently, the handoff passes the live reader set and its expiries, and the successor requires those readers' acknowledgements. (3) Apply the same `read_lease + ε` wait after an expiry takeover, so the rule needs no timing argument. It adds 0.5 s to reporting only, not to commits. (4) Extend DT-05 to handoff, and add the interleaving to the TLA+ model (AI-R6-2) |

## Decisions taken

None of these blocks the RFC. Each weighs buyers (including AI agents), suppliers, Share's
risk and operability.

| # | Question | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | RQ6-4: are minutes "not available" only for lack of probes valid evidence for a disaster? | **Endorse the proposal**: only measured failing minutes (at least 12 probes from at least two locations, under 90% success) count as evidence. Until the requirements change, keep the RFC's interim reading (accept the run, show counts, RB-4 forbids) | A probe-estate fault must never be able to exclude minutes. Auditability outweighs the rare case of an unmeasurable real disaster |
| D2 | RQ6-5: probe evidence followed by a recorded loss | **Endorse the RFC's interim reading** (count from the recorded loss), and support the proposal (the earlier of the two) for the next amendment | The interim reading counts more minutes against Share, never fewer, so it is safe |
| D3 | Kafka `min.insync.replicas` on a facility loss or facility maintenance | **Lower to 2** automatically (R6-12) | Kafka is transport. Facts are two-region in the database, and stopping feeds costs X2-S22 and X2-S17 minutes for no durability gain |
| D4 | Webhook batching | **Allowed**: a signed POST may carry up to 100 events, each with its own event ID. Receivers deduplicate per event. "Told" holds for each notice when its batch is first attempted (R6-15) | AI-agent buyers handle arrays easily, and mass notices must reach them within 60 s |
| D5 | Archive placement | **Cold tier on two providers' object storage in South Africa** (GCS plus one other), and **one Kenyan hot copy** for 13 months, unless legal (POL-24) requires a Kenyan cold copy (R6-14) | It removes most of the multi-PB on-premises operation from an 11 to 50 person team. The archive holds only ciphertext and no personal data (A11) |
| D6 | Partial outages invisible to the 90% minute | **Keep** POL-06 as the reported figure. **Add** an internal per-slot SLO with a page when any slot's probes all fail for 2 consecutive minutes | It gives buyers on a failing slot an operational response without changing the contractual measure before the requirements decide (RQ-R6-1) |
| D7 | Isolated (not lost) region | **Not declarable**, as RFC-000 R2-D2 says. Its minutes count | A fibre isolation is within Share's control (the go-live fibre condition) |
