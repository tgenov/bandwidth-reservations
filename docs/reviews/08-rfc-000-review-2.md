## Verdict

**ACCEPT WITH CHANGES.** There is no Blocker. RFC-001, RFC-002 and RFC-004 should not be
approved until A0R-01 … A0R-06 are resolved, either in this RFC or as binding rules handed
to those RFCs.

Both round-1 Blocker fixes are **safe**. Expiry is now a conditional log entry, the
first deciding entry wins, and participants release only on a committed non-confirmed
outcome. Under every crash point traced below, that gives no oversell, no double booking
and no confirmed reservation without its capacity. Merging the coordinator and the funds
into one account owner gives one decision point and one serial order per account. The
shape is still the cheapest one that meets the scenarios. I found no cheaper design that
meets them all.

The remaining problems fall into four groups:

1. **The RFC is designed against v1.2 readings that v1.3 decided differently.**
   - The confirm fence must be "deadline less completion margin". For partner routes that
     margin is 10 s, and the RFC confirms later than that (A0R-01).
   - Status reads must answer `pending` while the decision record is unavailable, not
     refuse (A0R-06).
2. **RQ-6 ("release durable before the outcome is reported") is claimed but not
   designed.** Two gaps follow from it:
   - While a link owner is unavailable, RQ-6 cannot be met together with `status_staleness`
     (A0R-02).
   - Delayed or retried hold commands can strand a hold behind a reported outcome (A0R-03).
3. **The home-account ledger split breaks exactly-once for payments** whose redelivery
   names another account (A0R-04).
4. **The topology's weak point is the Nairobi–Mombasa fibre, not the facilities.**
   - One cut may isolate Nairobi from Johannesburg as well.
   - Even when it does not, the cut makes Mombasa unavailable and Nairobi marginal against
     the 200 ms probe, and none of it counts as a declared disaster (A0R-05).

**Counts:** 0 Blocker, 6 Major, 8 Minor. 7 decisions taken. 5 requirements issues
(RQ-7 … RQ-11).

## Findings

| ID | Severity | Location | Finding | Proposed change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A0R-01 | **Major** | §4.3 "confirm fence"; §3 route sequence (entries 2–3); Requirements issues RQ-5; Benefits "Deadline" | **The confirm fence is not v1.3's.** POL-26 (v1.3) accepts a confirmation only if its entry is timestamped before `D` less the request's completion margin: `ack_budget` (200 ms) without a partner leg, `commit_margin` (10 s) with one (also the glossary *Outcome* and *Decision record*, P2-N3, P2 C20, P2-S104, P2-S106). The RFC uses `τ ≈ ε + δ_x`, "small so that 1 s deadlines stay usable". **(a) Without a partner leg** `τ` must be at least 200 ms. That is easy and costs nothing measurable. **(b) With a partner leg** the RFC lets committing begin as late as `D − commit_margin` (POL-26's own bullet) and appends the confirm one partner round trip later, so every late-committing partner route is confirmed after `D − commit_margin`: non-conformant (T8). The requirement contradicts itself: committing may begin at `D − 10 s`, yet must be decided before `D − 10 s`, which leaves `commit_margin` with no purpose (see P2-S95, "may not begin committing after 20 s"). | Set `τ = max(completion fence, ε + δ_x)`, with the state machine's guard at `D − τ`. Take the completion fence as `ack_budget` for every request, following RQ-7 (Decision R2-D1). If the business rejects RQ-7, RFC-002 must start committing no later than `D − commit_margin − partner_confirm_budget` and guard the confirm at `D − commit_margin`, which shortens every partner deadline by that budget. Restate §4.3 and the RQ-5 row. |
| A0R-02 | **Major** | §4.3 "Non-confirmed outcomes before `D`"; §3 deadline path; §4.3 `δ_x` | **RQ-6 is claimed but has no mechanism, and it conflicts with `status_staleness`.** (a) Status replicas and the notifier read the AO log, but nothing in that log records that the releases are durable. (b) If a link owner the request held on is electing a leader, the release cannot be durable within 1 s of the decision, yet P2-S99 requires the outcome from `status_staleness` after the decision (T7). In the same way, P2-S93 needs the release at `D` even when only the link's own log, not the decision record, is unavailable. (c) `δ_x` is said to cover "one commit and one notice (about 100 ms degraded)". The release at the link must also be committed by `D` (P2-S93): expiry commit + notice + release commit ≈ 25 + 5 + 27 ≈ 60 ms steady and 65 + 10 + 70 ≈ 145 ms degraded (est.), and `ε` comes on top. | Add a **"releases durable"** entry to the AO log, built from the link owners' release acknowledgements and batched into group commits. A non-confirmed outcome is reported (status replicas, outcome feed, webhook) only once that entry is applied. A new leader re-sends releases for every ending entry that has no such entry. State `δ_x` as two commits plus two hops plus margin, and keep `ack_budget ≥ τ > ε + δ_x` (with `ε` ≤ about 40 ms, est.). Raise RQ-8 for the conflict. |
| A0R-03 | **Major** | §3 (hold, release, yield, capacity released to a waiter); §4.4 internal-command identities ("an attempt number"); §8 link hold port | **Hold and release commands can cross, which strands holds and breaks RQ-6.** Delivery is at least once and unordered across retries (A6). (a) A late or retried *hold* applied after the request's *release* creates a hold for a request whose non-confirmed outcome may already be reported (T6). It lasts until the LO, seeing no outcome by `D`, asks the AO again. (b) Releases keyed by attempt number miss a hold re-granted after a yield (attempt n + 1) that the AO has not heard of yet. (c) The LO's "ask the AO to expire at `D`" is the only cleanup path, and its reply must also carry *confirmed* (convert); the RFC states only the failed case. | Make a release **request-scoped**: it ends every hold, waiter registration and grant the request has on that link, whatever the attempt. Keep a tombstone per request until `D` + `release_grace`, and refuse any hold or grant for a tombstoned request or one past `D`. The AO counts "releases durable" (A0R-02) only after acknowledgements from **every leg in the intake**, not only the legs it believes it holds. The answer to an expiry request carries the committed outcome, either way. RFC-001 and RFC-002 specify these as port invariants (§8). |
| A0R-04 | **Major** | §2 "Payments, receivables and reconciliation" (partition "by provider and account"); §2 ledger home-account rule; §4.4 "other money-moving inputs" | **Exactly-once for payments breaks across home partitions.** Payment events are partitioned by provider *and account*, and the ledger deduplicates only inside the movement's home partition. A redelivery whose contents differ in the account (a mistyped or corrected M-Pesa account reference) lands in another partition, where the reference is new, and is credited again. P4-N1 requires that it "moves no money and is reported". The same applies to any external reference whose content determines the home account (T12). | Partition payment events by **(provider, transaction reference)**. The first authenticated event binds the reference to its account, and the posting carries that binding; a later event with different contents is reported and moves nothing. This also makes orphan-reversal matching (72 h; "a reversal shares its payment's transaction reference") local to one partition. Internally generated references (payout instructions, operator actions, charge lines) are unique by construction and keep the home-account rule. |
| A0R-05 | **Major** | §3 RTT table and "Leader placement"; §5.1 failure row "The R1 ↔ R2 path"; R2, R14 | **The Nairobi–Mombasa fibre is a single point of availability, and the RFC models it as one link.** (a) The subsea cables to southern Africa land at Mombasa, so Nairobi's path to Johannesburg very likely backhauls over the same Nairobi–Mombasa routes, unless a terrestrial path through Tanzania is used. A cut then isolates R1 from both R2 and R3. R1 keeps 2 of 5 voters, loses quorum, and its leaders must move to R2 + R3. The RFC's row ("Leaders in R1 keep quorum through R3") is then wrong. (b) Even with a diverse R3 path, a cut makes R2 unavailable (forwarding through R3 adds about 95 ms RTT, so above 300 ms) and leaves R1 marginal (p50 about 170 ms, p99 about 215 ms, against a probe that succeeds under 200 ms in at least 90% of a minute's probes). This is not a declared disaster, so it counts against 99.99% (4.3 min a month), and a fibre repair takes hours. (c) The preference for R1 rests on an unmeasured 5 ms advantage (45 against 50 ms) that (a) may reverse. **The facility risk (R2) is acceptable:** with one facility in a metro, a facility loss becomes a declared disaster, and durability is unchanged because no region holds 3 voters. | Make fibre diversity a production gate in RFC-006 (Decision R2-D2): at least two Nairobi–Mombasa paths with no shared risk (no common duct, landing station or right of way), and R1 ↔ R3 and R2 ↔ R3 paths that do not ride either of them. Add an "R1 isolated" row to §5.1 (leaders move to R2; R1's edge is down). Choose the leader region by the measured RTT to R3 (R2-D7). Keep the degraded two-write single-link path (R13) as the only lever that brings a serving region back under 200 ms (est. 10 + 5 + 2 × 65 = 145 ms) while it commits through R3, and have RFC-006 evaluate it against this case, not only against a declared disaster. |
| A0R-06 | **Major** | §4.3 "status reads are unavailable, never wrong"; §4.6 | **Status reads while the record is unavailable contradict v1.3.** POL-04, P2-S106 and the glossary *Status read* say that while the decision record is unavailable at the deadline, status reads report `pending`, "never failed". The RFC answers "unavailable". There is also a gap in the replica rule: freshness gating is needed only for *undecided* answers. A committed deciding entry is final, so a replica that has applied it may always report it. | Replica rule: (1) a replica that has applied a deciding entry (and, for a non-confirmed outcome, its "releases durable" entry) reports it, however stale it is; (2) otherwise, with a closed position within `status_staleness` − ε, it reports `pending`; (3) otherwise, if `D` has passed and the replica has checked with the voters that no leader holds the lease, it reports `pending` (the POL-04 exception); (4) otherwise it refuses with retry. Rule (3) keeps a replica that is merely isolated from a healthy leader from reporting `pending` beyond `status_staleness`. RQ-10 asks that the exception not cover outcomes already reported. |
| A0R-07 | Minor | Header "Builds on"; Requirements issues ("New: RQ-5, RQ-6 … until the requirements decide"); §4.3 "conflicts with the literal text of P2-S104" | Not rebased on `requirements-v1.3`. RQ-5 was decided with the completion margin and `pending` (not the RFC's `τ` and "unavailable"); RQ-6 was decided as the RFC proposed. | Rebase on v1.3; move RQ-5 and RQ-6 to "Resolved" with pointers to review 07; apply A0R-01 and A0R-06; drop the P2-S104 sentence. |
| A0R-08 | Minor | §3 "The partner refuses definitively"; §2 partner adapters | (a) A re-ask is a partner hold that lasts until it is released (seconds) or lapses at `D`. Re-asking for a waiter that can no longer commit (past `D − commit_margin`, or already ended by its AO) blocks the next waiter within the guarantee (T11). (b) "One at a time per overlapping window" is transitive over chains of windows, so on a busy partner link it can serialise the whole link. (c) A hold expiry `D` is applied by the partner's clock: a confirm that reaches the partner after its `D` is answered "unknown" (C8) and must be reconciled and cancelled. | The PA skips waiters past their commit fence and those its AO has ended (it checks through the AO, or uses the deadline in the waiter entry). It serialises re-asks per pair of conflicting windows, not per chain. RFC-002 budgets partner clock skew inside `commit_margin` and states the reconcile-then-cancel path. |
| A0R-09 | Minor | §2 ledger, "otherwise Share's own account"; §4.2 "Aggregates remove the hot row" | Carried excess (per route and minute), the dearer-path difference (per reservation and minute), FX differences (per converted top-up and payout) and partner cancellation fees all touch no buyer or several suppliers, so they all go to **one** Share partition. That recreates the serial point the RFC claims to remove. | Home a movement that touches no buyer in the partition of the buyer whose request, reservation or traffic caused it, where there is one (the buyer's own balance stays wholly in its partition, because the movement does not touch it). Otherwise spread it over N Share sub-partitions by a hash of its source reference. Share's balance stays an aggregate at closed positions. |
| A0R-10 | Minor | §4.5 "Across ledger partitions"; §6 "one movement per account and minute" | (a) "Closed through T" holds only if a new ledger leader with a slower clock cannot append at T or before it: the posting time must be `max(clock, last closed T + 1 µs)`. (b) A per-account, per-minute movement carries many lines, each deduplicated by its own watermark. The RFC does not say that already-posted lines are dropped and only new ones posted, or that one conflicting line is excluded and reported without failing the whole movement. Without that, a re-composed batch after a settlement crash is refused or double-posted. | State both rules in §4.4 and §4.5; RFC-004 specifies them. |
| A0R-11 | Minor | §6 charge lines; R15 | Journal storage is not sized: about 1 M lines/s for 7 years is about 2.2 × 10¹⁴ lines, about 22 PB per copy at 100 B a line (est.), about four times the raw flow records the RFC does size. | Size it. Keep per-party totals in the movement, and the lines in sealed columnar segments (reservation, minute and amount, delta-encoded; about 10 B a line, so about 2 PB, est.) that still answer P4-N16 and P3-N9. RFC-004 confirms. |
| A0R-12 | Minor | §5.1 failure table, "Region R2 (declared disaster)" and "Region R1"; R13 ("replacement site") | With one Kenyan region lost, exactly 3 of 5 voters survive, so every commit needs all of them, and one more node, facility or R3-zone fault stops every write, while POL-26 outcomes by the deadline still apply (T15). | On declaration, reconfigure each group to **2 + 2**: the surviving Kenyan region's two voters and two R3 voters in different GCP zones. This is the cheap "replacement" the RFC leaves open; no third site is needed (R2-D3). |
| A0R-13 | Minor | §3 "Placement fails leaders over region-wide"; §5.1; R2 | Region-wide failover is not specified: what decides that "R1 can no longer lead", the placement map's own quorum, and how thousands of groups re-elect leaders after the lease waits within admission's 1 min RTO. | RFC-006 specifies the placement log (2 + 2 + 1), the trigger, staggered elections and the expected time to move every leader, and tests it in the R1 spike. |
| A0R-14 | Minor | §3 entry 2 (committing mark) | Probably over-engineered. ## Decisions taken

| # | Decision | Balance |
|---|---|---|
| R2-D1 | **One confirm fence for every request: `D − τ`, `τ = max(ack_budget, ε + δ_x)`**, i.e. 200 ms with the current estimates. The design follows RQ-7's amended reading of POL-26 for partner routes (a confirmation stamped before `D − ack_budget` counts; committing still begins by `D − commit_margin`). | Partner routes keep the full `commit_margin` for the partner confirm, as POL-26 intends. Single-link and Share-only routes lose nothing. If RQ-7 is rejected, partner deadlines shrink by a confirm budget (A0R-01). |
| R2-D2 | **Fibre diversity is a production gate; a fibre cut is not a declared disaster.** Diverse paths, with no shared risk, between Nairobi and Mombasa and towards Johannesburg are a precondition, verified by Share's network team and recorded in RFC-006. An isolated serving region counts against availability, as POL-06 already says. | Honest to buyers in the isolated region. The cost is fibre capex and the burden of proving diversity, rather than relaxing the target. |
| R2-D3 | **2 + 2 reconfiguration during a declared region disaster** (A0R-12). | Restores single-fault tolerance with no new site, with two-region durability intact. The cost is one extra R3 voter per group while the disaster lasts. |
| R2-D4 | **Replica status rule as in A0R-06**, including the "no leader holds the lease" check before answering `pending` past `status_staleness`. | Conforms to P2-S99 and P2-S106. A replica cut off from a healthy leader refuses instead of reporting `pending` for longer than allowed. |
| R2-D5 | **Request-scoped releases with tombstones until `D` + `release_grace`** (A0R-03). | One small set per link, bounded by the requests in flight (about 190 on the hottest link, plus those within a minute after their deadline). It closes every crossing of a hold and a release. |
| R2-D6 | **Payment events keyed by (provider, transaction reference)**; Share-only movements homed by the causing buyer, otherwise by a hash of the source reference (A0R-04, A0R-09). | Exactly-once by construction, with no hot Share partition. Payments lose locality by account, which nothing relies on. |
| R2-D7 | **The leader region is chosen by measurement**: R1 by default until the spike (R1) measures each Kenyan region's RTT to R3, then the nearer one. | A 5 ms assumption should not fix the placement. |
