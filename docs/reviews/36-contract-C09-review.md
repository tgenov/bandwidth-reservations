## Verdict

**REJECT** (revise and re-review). The review finds 1 Blocker, 12 Majors and 18 Minors
(31 findings).

The payload path (`keep_by`, withdrawal, tombstones, the feed-before-answer rule) holds
under the crash and ambiguity traces below, apart from R36-05. The subject-item side is too
loose for a store whose threat model includes a compromised console:

- `put` does not limit which item kinds a caller may write.
- `put` lets any allowlisted caller write a new version of any existing item.
- `get` has no purpose-to-kind binding.

So a compromised console can redirect payouts and read every KYB and bank document. The
restore path that S-M2 relies on has no defined write path, cutoff or feed retention. The
erasure and expiry bounds also leak in three places: puts after an erasure, unvalidated
`received_at`, and late execution.

### Traces run

1. **Put answer lost, then a withdrawal.** The put fixes its timestamp T ≤ `keep_by` − ε
   through `cluster_logical_timestamp()`. Its commit RPC stalls past the caller's
   `keep_by` + ε. The caller withdraws, and the withdrawal reads the item key at W > T.
   - It meets the put's intent and waits for it or pushes it.
   - A pushed put cannot move its fixed timestamp, so it aborts and answers `late`.
   - Either the put commits first and the withdrawal deletes it, or the put aborts.

   Safe. The claim "no commit after `keep_by` − ε" holds for the commit timestamp, not for
   wall time (a commit can complete seconds later at a low timestamp). The withdrawal
   covers that case, so nothing is wrong, but RFC-003 §3.3's crash-table wording ("no item
   can appear after the withdrawal") depends on serialisability, not on wall time.
2. **Ambiguous commit at the store** (CockroachDB `AmbiguousResultError` on the put's
   commit). The contract does not say what the store answers, or what the caller does with
   `temporarily_unavailable`. This fails: R36-05.
3. **Erase racing a put of the same subject.** The put commits after the erase. "Subject
   key created on first put" re-creates the subject, and its data outlives an executed
   request. This fails: R36-04.
4. **Non-user request for days D…D+3, executed on D+2.** Payloads of x1 later on D+2 and on
   D+3 go into a bucket key version created after the erasure and stay readable. This
   fails X1-S42: R36-09.
5. **Restore from a 6-day-old backup.** The tooling must re-apply erasures, but it has no
   write operation. Re-applying through `erase` or `withdraw_payload` finds no idempotency
   record in the restored store and emits duplicate events. An erasure whose event is
   still in identity's outbox (relay lag) is missed. This fails: R36-06.
6. **Erasure executed on day 20** (the incident threshold). Generation h is destroyed at
   e + 14 = day 34 > `erasure_max`. This fails: R36-10.
7. **A compromised console puts `payout_destination_details` under an existing
   (`destination_ref`, version) item.** The next C16 read returns the attacker's account,
   which then appears on an instruction. This fails: R36-01.

## Decisions taken

- **D-36-1 (purpose → kinds, R36-03).** Support sees names, contacts and display names,
  never KYB documents, legal-order documents or bank details. Compliance reads case notes
  and documents under the `case_note` purpose. Finance and support read payloads under
  `flow_record_dispute`.
  - Why: this balances support's need to identify callers against exposure of the most
    sensitive items, and buyers' and suppliers' trust.
  - A KYB review purpose can be added later (additive).
- **D-36-2 (notices, R36-26).** Supplier administrators are told of destroyed hours only
  for `exporters_period` scopes.
  - Why: the supplier needs the misconfiguration signal for non-user data. For its own
    users the notice adds nothing and can identify the requester.
- **D-36-3 (payloads in an erased period, R36-09).** Payloads received in a named period
  after the erasure are not kept (`not_kept(erased)`), until the period ends.
  - Why: the person asked for exactly that, and the loss is dispute evidence in a case that
    is already the supplier's misconfiguration (POL-19's own reasoning).
- **D-36-4 (late erasure, R36-10).** A late erasure forces an early generation rotation,
  at the cost of one extra rewrap (under 2 minutes, est., RFC-005 §3.7). This is cheaper
  than missing `erasure_max`.
- **D-36-5 (caps, R36-08).** If exact "consumed only when kept" accounting proves costly,
  caps may count admitted attempts. POL-19 requires only stated caps, and the difference
  favours the store's protection.

## Requirements issues

- **RQ-36-1 (erasure-request evidence).** A non-user's erasure request is kept, with its
  evidence of identity, as a case note tagged with the request's `subject_id`. The request
  scope (RFC-005 §3.7) does not include that note, so the person's ID evidence outlives
  their executed erasure indefinitely. POL-19 says nothing on retaining erasure-request
  evidence.
  - Proposed: keep it for the audit retention needed to prove the request (legal confirms
    per market, POL-24), or erase it with the request after a stated period.
- **RQ-36-2 (payout destination of a sole trader).** Payout destination details may be a
  natural person's bank or mobile-money account. POL-19 erasure (of a supplier user)
  versus POL-22 and the 7-year payout records does not say whether those details are
  erased, kept for the records' life, or replaced by a reference.
  - Proposed: keep them while any payout under the version may be reconciled, then erase
    them on a covering request. State it in POL-19.
- **RQ-36-3 (future payloads under a user's request).** POL-19's "the request of a user of
  the supplier whose exporter sent it" covers a payload with no time limit. It is unclear
  whether payloads received after execution are covered. RFC-005 reads it as "received
  before the erasure".
  - Proposed: confirm that reading in POL-19. Contrast the named-period case, D-36-3.

## Architecture issues

- **AI-36-1 (RFC-000 §8 personal-data store row).** Proposed amendment:
  - put idempotency by (subject, item, version), with write-once kinds (R36-01, R36-02);
  - the storage cap (S-M1) and the `expiry_destroyed` event (S-M2);
  - the `GetPurpose` table with purpose → kinds (S-G2, R36-03);
  - "payloads not kept are counted from ingestion's log and reported hourly to network
    operations; the store's own counts reconcile only" (replacing "counted per exporter
    and minute and reported");
  - a restore-only `reapply` operation for RFC-006 (R36-06).
- **AI-36-2 (RFC-005 §3.7 erasure bound).** Proposed amendment:
  - force a new `KEK_PD` generation for any erasure committed after r + 9 days;
  - move `erasure_incident` from 20 to 14 days;
  - state the bound as min(r + 24, e + 14) days, and e + 7 days after a forced rotation
    (R36-10).
