# Review 01: adversarial review of the requirements baseline (P1–P4)

| | |
|---|---|
| Scope | `01-discovery/` (company profile, P1–P4 problem statements) and `02-specs/*.feature` |
| Primary source | The Share job listing (`job-specs/share.txt`), plus the company profile as secondary evidence |
| Deliberately not read | Code, tests, SQL, RFCs, delivery docs, history log |
| Date | 2026-10-11 |
| Stance | Adversarial: the aim is to find what would mislead an RFC or an implementation if this baseline were made authoritative |

## Verdict

**Not fit to be authoritative in its current form.** The problem statements are mostly
sound. They state needs, mark assumptions honestly and leave the policy questions open.
The specs do not hold to that standard. Large parts of them, P2 above all, but also P3's
transport scenarios and P4's ledger and split scenarios, describe how one prototype
behaves rather than what a buyer, supplier, operator or finance team needs. They lock in
holds, commit points, age-based tickets, wound-wait, watermarks, Kafka topic names, HTTP
paths, database error strings, micro-unit rounding and one particular apportionment
method. Several of these quietly answer open questions that the problem statements say
the business owns: who wins under contention, whether to resume after a crash, which
minute a long flow is billed to, how unknown records are handled, and how rounding ties
are broken. In at least one place (P4-S18) the requirement was weakened to match what a
test asserts. Verification status, test names and defect notes sit inside the
requirements, so "authoritative" would come to mean "whatever the prototype's tests check".
There are also gaps a marketplace that holds money cannot ship without: authorization and
tenant isolation, non-functional targets (99.99%, ×50, durability and recovery), the
reservation lifecycle (cancel, modify, end, supply loss), and a business answer to whether
reserved but unused capacity costs the buyer anything.

**What must change first, in order:**

1. Remove all verification metadata from the `.feature` files and move it to a conformance
   matrix (R-01).
2. Rewrite P2 as observable outcomes. Move hold, commit-point and ticket mechanics to
   RFC-002 (R-03, R-08).
3. Have the business answer the five Blocker decisions: D-01 (is reserved capacity
   charged), D-02 (the contention policy), D-05 (single-link versus in-flight route),
   D-06 (NFR targets) and D-13 (long flows).
4. Add the authorization and tenant-isolation requirements (R-30).
5. Turn the policy choices the specs have already made into named, parameterised business
   decisions, each marked "default, pending decision D-nn" until a decision is taken.

Until then, an RFC can satisfy this baseline only by copying the prototype. That is the
risk this review was asked to find.

## Decision register

Each question is for the product owner or the business. Until it is answered, the spec
should carry the stated default as a named parameter marked "pending D-nn", never as an
unmarked requirement.

- **D-01 (R-02).** Does holding a confirmed reservation cost the buyer anything when it carries no traffic? Options: (a) pure pay-per-use, where reserved capacity is free and the hoarding risk is accepted; (b) a reservation fee per Mbps-minute reserved, with usage charged on top; (c) a minimum charge per reservation or per period; (d) a usage floor: idle reservations are released, or their capacity becomes pre-emptible, after a stated idle time; (e) different models per product or customer segment.
- **D-02 (R-04).** When requests compete for the same capacity, which policy decides who wins, and must it be changeable at run time? Options: (a) age of the request (oldest wins), the prototype's behaviour; (b) value (whole routes over single links, or larger or longer bookings first); (c) customer tier; (d) first to finish; any of these subject to the fixed invariants (never oversell, at least one competitor wins, no starvation).
- **D-03 (R-05).** If Share's booking process fails while a route request is in progress, should the request be resumed or failed? Options: (a) fail it, give back what it held, and tell the buyer; (b) resume it automatically within its deadline; (c) resume only if it can still finish within a stated time, else fail it.
- **D-04 (R-06).** What does a buyer see while a route request is in progress, and how do they learn the outcome? Options: (a) synchronous answer only, blocking up to the route budget; (b) immediate "pending" plus polling by idempotency key; (c) "pending" plus a webhook; (d) both (b) and (c). Also: what is the deadline for the first "pending" acknowledgement?
- **D-05 (R-07).** When a single-link request fits only if an in-progress route request fails, what does the single-link buyer get, and which component is the single authority for a link's schedule? Options: (a) immediate rejection with a "capacity in flight, retry after T" reason; (b) wait up to the 200 ms budget, then reject; (c) treat it as pending, like a route; (d) single-link requests take priority over in-flight route holds.
- **D-06 (R-10).** What are the non-functional targets? Specifically: availability (is 99.99% for admission, for billing, for payments, or for all; per region or global?); throughput at the ×50 target (admission requests/s overall and on the hottest link; flow records/s or bytes/day metered; webhooks/s); latency (single-link p99, route p99, top-up to credited balance, minute end to first charge, suspension decision to enforcement); durability and recovery (RPO and RTO for reservations, the ledger and raw usage); retention of raw usage and ledger history. Options per item: adopt the profile's figures (99.99%, ×50 of 45 TB/day, 500 req/s, 200 ms) as targets, or set different ones.
- **D-07 (R-11).** When a link's capacity falls below what is confirmed on it (fault, maintenance, partner reduction, supplier exit), what happens to the reservations that no longer fit? Options: (a) nothing is revoked; affected reservations are flagged and SLA credits apply (Problem 6); (b) reservations are degraded pro rata; (c) reservations are revoked in a stated order with notice; (d) planned maintenance must be booked in advance as a reservation against the link. In every option, must new sales on that link stop at the reduced capacity?
- **D-08 (R-12).** Which reservation changes may a buyer make, and on what terms? Options for each of cancel, shorten, extend, increase rate, decrease rate and renew: (a) allowed at any time with effect from now; (b) allowed with a notice period; (c) allowed only before the reservation starts; (d) not allowed (book a new one). Also: may a route be partly cancelled, and who pays a partner's cancellation fee?
- **D-09 (R-13).** How far ahead does the marketplace sell, and what does an open-ended reservation guarantee as that limit moves forward? Options: (a) a fixed rolling selling limit, with open-ended bookings automatically extended and guaranteed as it moves; (b) open-ended bookings guaranteed only up to the limit at the time of purchase, then renewed subject to capacity; (c) open-ended bookings guaranteed indefinitely, with the limit applying only to new bookings with a set end.
- **D-10 (R-14).** What must a rejection tell the buyer so they can act on it? Options (any combination): (a) the largest rate that fits the requested window; (b) the nearest window that fits the requested rate; (c) the sub-ranges that are too full; (d) the link (for routes) that blocked the request; (e) a "retry after" time when capacity is in flight. Should rejections disclose other buyers' aggregate usage at all?
- **D-11 (R-17).** What is the money unit and rounding policy? Options: (a) one internal unit finer than any currency (for example micros), with each charge rounded once and a rule for converting to the provider's unit at top-up and payout; (b) each currency's own minor unit throughout; (c) a per-currency precision table. For rounding: half up, half even, or always in the buyer's favour? Where does the sub-unit residue go: carried forward per account, or to Share?
- **D-12 (R-18).** Which apportionment rule splits a charge among parties, and how far may a share deviate from its exact proportion? Options: (a) a monotone divisor method that favours larger parties (as in the prototype); (b) a monotone method that keeps every share within one smallest unit of its exact proportion (for example quota-capped methods); (c) carry the fractional remainder forward per party so that cumulative shares stay exact over time. How are ties broken: by account name, by a fixed published order, or in Share's favour? Must the rule be published to suppliers?
- **D-13 (R-19).** How are the bytes of a flow that spans several minutes, a price change, or the start or end of a reservation charged? Options: (a) all to the minute in which the flow ended, at that minute's price; (b) spread evenly across the minutes the flow was active, each at its own price; (c) require exporters to cut flows at minute boundaries (active timeout ≤ 60 s) so the question does not arise; (d) (a) for flows shorter than N minutes, (b) above it.
- **D-14 (R-20).** What lateness and enforcement times may the exposure bound assume, and what happens when they are exceeded? Options: (a) a declared maximum record lateness, with records later than that billed anyway but reported as bound breaches; (b) records later than the maximum are not billed (loss accepted); (c) the bound includes a margin for price rises within the window (or price rises take effect only after the window). And under a credit limit: is the limit the most the customer may owe *including* the unmetered window (suspend early) or *excluding* it (loss up to limit + exposure)?
- **D-15 (R-21).** Which buyers are subject to which zero-balance policy, given "no upfront payment"? Options: (a) suspend at zero with the exposure held back in the wallet (an effective deposit); (b) a credit limit per customer, with the loss accepted up to it; (c) (a) for new or unverified customers, (b) for vetted enterprises; (d) post-paid invoicing for enterprises. Is refusing a new reservation for insufficient funds acceptable under "no upfront payment"?
- **D-16 (R-22).** When a customer is suspended, what happens to their traffic and reservations, and what happens when they top up? Options for reservations: (a) kept, with capacity held; (b) kept for a grace period, then released; (c) released at once. Options for resumption: (a) automatic once headroom is restored; (b) automatic after a minimum top-up; (c) manual review.
- **D-17 (R-24).** How is traffic that cannot be matched to a valid, active reservation handled? This covers unknown reservation IDs, traffic before start or after end or cancellation, traffic above the reserved rate, and traffic during suspension. Options: (a) kept and billed to the account at the standard or a penalty rate; (b) kept but not billed, reported to operations and the supplier; (c) kept, billed, and the over-rate traffic counted against the exposure bound. Who pays the supplier for unbilled traffic on their link?
- **D-18 (R-27).** What balance does a buyer see? Options: (a) the ledger balance only, with a note that recent usage is not yet charged; (b) the ledger balance plus an estimate of usage not yet charged, shown as "available"; (c) available headroom under the customer's policy (balance minus exposure, or plus credit limit).
- **D-19 (R-29).** Must the system be able to answer "what was known to be reserved on this link at time T" for any past T? Options: (a) yes, full history of every reservation change with who made it and when (bitemporal); (b) current state plus an append-only change log, reconstructable on demand; (c) current state only, with history kept by billing and SLA systems. For how long?
- **D-20 (R-31).** Which currencies are supported, and how? Options: (a) one currency per wallet, with suppliers paid in the buyer's currency; (b) one currency per wallet, with suppliers paid in their own currency and Share carrying FX risk between charge and payout; (c) a settlement currency (for example USD), with conversion at top-up and at payout; (d) multi-currency wallets. Is pricing per currency, or converted from one base price, and at what rate source and time?
- **D-21 (R-32).** How are reversals, refunds and disputes handled? Questions: may a buyer withdraw unused balance, and how? What happens when a reversal arrives after the top-up was spent (negative balance: suspend, collect, write off)? Is a reversal that arrives without its completion held pending or applied at once? Do SLA credits go into the wallet as money or as non-withdrawable credit? Options for each: in scope now, in scope later (record the requirement), or explicitly out of scope.
- **D-22 (R-33).** What is a payout and when does it happen? Options: frequency (daily, weekly, monthly, on demand); minimum payout amount; maximum time a supplier's clawback debt may remain open before it is collected another way (invoice, offset across currencies, write-off); whether a payout is "done" when instructed or when the bank or mobile-money transfer is confirmed.
- **D-23 (R-34).** Who bears the loss when a buyer's negative balance is never paid? Options: (a) Share alone, with supplier earnings final at charge time; (b) suppliers pro rata, by clawback, with notice; (c) Share's commission first, then suppliers; (d) supplier earnings become final only when the buyer's wallet covered the charge.
- **D-24 (R-37).** Is holding prepaid buyer funds and paying suppliers from them a regulated activity in Kenya and in each planned market, and what does that require (licence or partner institution, safeguarding or segregated accounts, buyer KYC/AML, reconciliation frequency, record retention, refund rights)? Options: (a) operate under a licensed payment partner; (b) obtain the licences; (c) restructure so Share does not hold funds (for example direct payment to suppliers).
- **D-25 (R-39).** Are AI-agent controls and abuse limits in scope now, and owned by which problem? Options: (a) in scope in P2 and P4 now (spending limits per agent key, expiring quotes, rate limits, caps on in-flight capacity per account); (b) a separate problem P5 with its own statement; (c) out of scope for this baseline, with the risk recorded.
- **D-26 (R-42).** How long may an unfinished route request keep capacity in flight, and what is the route deadline? Options: (a) a single marketplace-wide value (for example 60 s); (b) per link or per partner, according to partner hold lifetimes; (c) per request, chosen by the buyer within a maximum.
- **D-27 (R-44).** Can a price change take effect mid-minute, and if so which price applies to that minute? Options: (a) prices change only on minute boundaries (the question goes away); (b) the price in force at the start of the minute; (c) the lower of the two (buyer-favourable); (d) pro rata by seconds.
- **D-28 (R-46).** How are partner links without holds, and routes over several partners, booked all or nothing? Options: (a) book no-hold partners last, after every other leg is held, accepting a compensating cancellation (and its fee, borne by Share) if a later step fails; (b) do not sell routes through no-hold partners; (c) sell them with a buyer-visible "best effort, may be cancelled within T" term; (d) negotiate holds as a condition of listing.

- No recorded money movement can be altered or removed by any actor, including operators; attempts are refused and recorded (replaces P4-S10 to S12)
- A reversal after the top-up was spent is handled by the decided rule (D-21)
- A reversal whose completion never arrives is handled by the decided rule (D-21)
- A buyer can withdraw unused balance, or is told they cannot (D-21)
- A supplier can see each charge's share and the rule and weights that produced it (R-28)
- A payout run repeated or retried pays each supplier at most once (R-33)
- A supplier's clawback debt older than the limit is handled by the decided rule (D-22)
- Bad debt from an unpaid negative balance is borne by the decided party (D-23)
- The ledger is reconciled against each provider's settlement report, and differences are reported for resolution (P4 OQ6)
- Every amount carries a currency, and a conversion is a recorded movement at a recorded rate (D-20)
- The sub-unit residue from conversion and rounding is accounted for (D-11)
- A notification redelivered with different contents is reported and moves no money (already P4-S05; keep, mark as decided by the business)

### Cross-cutting

- Every operation that moves money or capacity records the authenticated actor (R-30)
- An operator action on another account's reservation or wallet requires the operator role and is recorded (R-30)
- Availability, durability and recovery targets per D-06, each with a measurement method

## Cross-cutting recommendations

1. **Move verification status out of the requirements.** The `.feature` files should state
   required behaviour only, with stable scenario IDs. Put conformance in a separate,
   regenerable matrix (`04-delivery/conformance.md`, or a report generated from test
   annotations that cite scenario IDs): scenario ID → RFC section → test(s) → status
   (met / partly met / not met) → notes and known defects. A scenario that is "met on one
   path only" is *partly met*, never verified. Change the `README.md` convention to match.
   This also removes every link from the baseline to test names and decision-log IDs.

2. **Split each spec into a need-level layer and a design-level layer.** The `02-specs`
   files hold only buyer-, supplier-, operator- and finance-observable behaviour. Design
   invariants (hold accounting, commit-point recovery, wound-wait, watermarks, minute
   locks, divisor-method outputs) become acceptance tests attached to the RFC that chose
   them, each citing the need-level scenario it serves. An RFC that chooses a different
   mechanism replaces those tests but still satisfies the baseline.

3. **Add a policy register.** Every business choice the system must support becomes a
   named parameter with an owner, a current value, and the D-number that set it: contention
   policy, zero-balance policy per segment, resume rule, rounding rule, apportionment rule
   and tie rule, maximum lateness, enforcement time, in-flight time limit, route deadline,
   selling limit, rejection-reason content. Specs refer to a parameter by name ("under the
   account's zero-balance policy") and never hard-code its value. Scenarios that illustrate
   one value say "with policy X set to Y".

4. **Write one NFR specification** (for example `02-specs/NFR.md` or `NFR.feature`), in
   one place rather than scattered: availability per capability; latency per operation
   with a load profile (overall, and on the hottest link); throughput at today's load and
   at the ×50 target; durability (RPO) and recovery (RTO) for reservations, raw usage and
   the ledger; data retention; and the measurement method for each. Each target cites
   D-06.

5. **Add a glossary** (`docs/glossary.md`) and enforce it. At minimum: buyer, account,
   supplier, partner, link, route, reservation, reserved rate, traffic, window, selling
   limit (replacing "horizon"), confirmed, rejected, invalid, pending, failed, capacity in
   flight, charge, amendment, exposure, headroom, standing, suspension, top-up, reversal,
   payout, clawback, smallest money unit. Fix one time notation and one unit convention
   (rates in Mbps, volumes in bytes or GB = 10⁹ bytes, money with an explicit currency).
   Resolve the "usage" collision first.

6. **Add a cross-cutting security and tenancy rule set** (authentication, per-account
   isolation, idempotency keys scoped per account, operator roles, an actor recorded on
   every money or capacity change, webhook authenticity per provider). Every problem
   inherits it.

7. **Own the seams explicitly.** Name one owner for each requirement that spans problems:
   admission funds check (P1/P2 ↔ P3/P4), suspension (P3 ↔ P4 ↔ network operations),
   single-link versus route capacity (P1 ↔ P2), reservation end and cancellation versus
   billing (P1/P2 ↔ P3). A short `02-specs/seams.md`, or a `Rule` in the owning feature,
   avoids today's duplicates (P3-S02 and P4-S15) and orphans (the funds refusal in P3-S21).

8. **Re-mark assumption statuses** against a stated meaning of "Confirmed" (the only
   primary sources are the job listing and a mock interview). The billing stack and the
   double-entry ledger should not be *Confirmed*.

9. **Fill in the lifecycle and failure surface before the RFCs are approved:** the
   reservation lifecycle (D-08, D-09), supply loss (D-07), unmatched traffic (D-17), money
   reversals and refunds (D-21), payouts and bad debt (D-22, D-23), and abuse limits
   (D-25). Each can be explicitly out of scope, but it must be *listed* as out of scope
   with an owner, not simply absent.
