## Verdict

**PASS WITH FIXES.** The round-3/4 rewrite closes both Blockers from round 1. The contention
guarantee is now scoped the same way in POL-02, the glossary, P2-N4, C4 and P1. The priority
rule no longer pre-empts. Deadlines and `commit_margin` agree. Every need is cited, every
`@need-` tag resolves, scenario IDs are unique, each file has one `Feature`, and every Outline
placeholder has its column. Arithmetic checks out on every row in scope except one expected
value (GA2-04). No implementation leakage or verification metadata is left.

Nothing below makes the baseline unsatisfiable. Eight Major defects must still be fixed
before the baseline is authoritative:

- The pairwise crossing rule does not stop a three-way cycle, so the guarantee fails there (GA2-01).
- One kind of partner refusal is in fact contention caused by Share itself (GA2-02).
- P1-N1 contradicts the unplanned-outage rule (GA2-03).
- One counter-offer's expected answer is wrong under the policy text (GA2-04).
- The set of reservations an outage affects is defined three different ways (GA2-05).
- Two glossary entries still state round-3 rules that round 4 replaced (GA2-06, GA2-07).
- Five policy questions are still open (GA2-08).

Every fix is a rewording, a tag or a scenario, applied under the decisions below.

## Decisions taken

| # | Question | Decision | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-01 | How are contention cycles of three or more requests broken? (GA2-01) | A younger request that holds capacity an older request is waiting for, and is itself waiting, releases that capacity and waits within its deadline. A pairwise crossing is the two-request case. A holder that is not waiting is still never pre-empted | Breaks every cycle at once without detecting cycles across links. A waiting younger request can give up a hold that was not in a cycle, but it keeps its place among waiters |
| D-02 | Is a partner's definite refusal caused by Share's own in-flight hold at that partner a contention loss? (GA2-02) | Yes. While another Share request holds overlapping capacity at the partner, the refused request waits within its deadline as on a Share link, then asks again. Other definite refusals still reject | Keeps the guarantee where Share caused the contention, at the cost of extra partner calls and longer waits on hot partner links |
| D-03 | Earliest window in a counter-offer: from now, or from the requested start? (GA2-04) | At or after the requested start, within the selling horizon | Matches what the buyer asked for and makes answers deterministic. An earlier free slot is not offered |
| D-04 | Which reservations does an unplanned outage affect, and for how long? (GA2-05) | Reduced capacity applies from the outage's record until its restoration, open-ended. New sales are decided against it. Reservations are affected at the minutes where committed > reduced capacity, all of them at those minutes (no newest-first). An outage that leaves capacity ≥ committed affects none. Holders active at the record are told within `affected_notice_time`. A reservation that starts during the outage is told within `affected_notice_time` of its start | No false alarms, and the network-cannot-choose rule holds. Long-dated sales on the link pause until restoration, and holders of later windows learn of the outage only when it reaches them |
| D-05 | What can a supplier do about maintenance that needs sold capacity? (P1 open question 2) | Not bookable as maintenance. The supplier waits for capacity to free, or announces a permanent reduction with `revocation_notice`. Works carried out anyway are recorded as an unplanned outage on the link, attributed to the supplier for SLA purposes (Problem 6) | Buyer guarantees and notice terms stay intact. The supplier bears the consequences of its own works |
| D-06 | Partner reconciliation interval and who resolves a mismatch (P2 open question 1) | Any partner call with an unknown result is reconciled for that booking at once, by idempotent re-query. Every partner is fully reconciled daily, by 12:00 UTC on D + 1 (as POL-24). Each mismatch alerts operations. A partner booking with no Share reservation behind it is cancelled at Share's cost. A Share reservation that the partner lacks is treated as affected by an unplanned outage on that leg | Bounded divergence, and the buyer is never charged for Share's or the partner's error. The cost is a daily batch per partner and the occasional partner fee Share pays |
| D-07 | How is hold-squatting across many accounts bounded? (P2 open question 3, GA-22) | A request takes no capacity hold until its funds hold or credit hold is taken. Both are released at its outcome if it is not confirmed. KYC tiers are not relied on | Squatting costs real prepaid money in every account, whatever the number of accounts. A request blocked on capacity briefly ties up the buyer's funds |
| D-08 | What may a supplier see of buyers on its link? (X1 open question 1) | The reservation, its rate, window and endpoints, and an opaque per-reservation reference for provisioning. Not the buyer's account identity, unless the buyer consents or a legal order requires it | Carrier neutrality and discretion, and suppliers cannot take buyers off-platform. Provisioning works on the reference |
| D-09 | Which operator roles exist? (X1 open question 2) | Support: reads, and acts only at the account's documented request. Network operations: records outages and restorations, and terminates for network abuse. Finance: price corrections and quarantine attribution. Compliance: terminates for fraud or a legal order, with a second compliance operator's approval. Each action belongs to exactly one role | Clear accountability, and four eyes on the irreversible terminations that are not urgent. Abuse can still be stopped by one operator at once |
| D-10 | When part of a reservation is affected by a permanent reduction, what is the equivalent path offered for? (GA2-09) | The whole reservation at its full rate. If accepted, all of it moves. If declined or lapsed, only the affected Mbps are removed | Keeps a route one unit at one rate, and Share bears the price difference on more Mbps |
| D-11 | What is a request's outcome when Share is down past its deadline? (GA2-12) | A request not confirmed by its deadline has failed as of its deadline, whatever Share's state, and later status reports that. Capacity held for it lapses at the deadline on every leg | Capacity is never stranded by an outage of Share's own booking process. Holds must expire on their own |
| D-12 | What does a request that drops out of the contention guarantee get back? (GA2-13) | A waiter failed at its deadline gets the reason "deadline passed while waiting" and a `retry_after` of the remaining deadline of the request ahead. A `wait = false` rejection also carries the POL-10 counter-offer, computed beside the capacity in flight | Agents get a machine-actionable next step without stretching the guarantee. The response grows slightly |

Each decision is provisional, like the rest of the register, and is to be written to the

POL-07: D-04, D-05, D-10; POL-28: D-06; POL-25: D-07; POL-03 and POL-26: D-11; new entries,
