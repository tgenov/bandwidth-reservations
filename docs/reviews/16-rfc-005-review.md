## Verdict

**Not ready: revise to revision 3.** 2 Blockers, 12 Majors, 14 Minors (28 findings).

The shape is sound and mostly well argued: authenticating at the edge from pushed caches,
taking the tenant only from the credential, deciding once at the owner, keeping audit in the
owner's own row, and keeping personal data out of every log. Most X1 scenarios are met. The
RFC fails the baseline in two places:

1. **Erasure.** It is still written against `requirements-v1.6`. `requirements-v1.7` sets
   `erasure_max` to 30 days *from the request*, across the live store, backups and escrow
   (POL-19, X1-N11, X1-S39, RFC-000 A11, §5.3, §8). The RFC promises 44 days *from the
   erasure commit*, and its claim that the live store is unreadable at commit is false on
   CockroachDB (R5-01). The v1.7 request path is not designed at all, and X1-S39 is
   missing from the traceability (R5-02).
2. **Stronger guarantees than the mechanisms give.** Several security claims go beyond
   what the mechanism delivers. A compromised console can still get operators to sign
   actions they did not see, can ignore cancellations and expiry, and can read across
   tenants without leaving a record (R5-07, R5-08). Symmetric AEAD with keys released to
   consumers does not prove who wrote a row (R5-09). Rows outside the log tables
   (projections, system ranges) are neither confidential from R3 nor integrity-protected
   against a database administrator (R5-11). The 60 s revocation has no path that works
   without Kafka at the account owner (R5-05).

None of this needs a new component or principle. The fixes are parameters, contract text,
some stronger bindings, and a few reads and feeds that must be named in RFC-000 §8.

## Findings

Severity: **Blocker** (the baseline is not met, or a security claim is false in a way that
matters, and the RFC cannot be accepted as it is); **Major** (a requirement or contract is
at risk, or a stated guarantee is materially overstated; must be fixed in revision 3);
**Minor** (hardening, precision or simplification).

### Blockers

| ID | Severity | Location | Finding | Proposed change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R5-01 | Blocker | §3.7 (Erasure table, Generations), §10 (key table, Escrow), §13 (`kek_generation_period`, `pd_backup_retention`), C12, RK-13, Benefits, RQ5-11 | **The erasure bound breaks `erasure_max`, and the live-store claim is false.** (a) v1.7 POL-19, X1-N11, X1-S39 and RFC-000 A11, §5.3 and §8 require every copy (live store, backups, escrow) to be unrecoverable within `erasure_max` = 30 days **of the request**. The RFC gives 30 + 14 = 44 days **from the erasure commit**, and adds the time from request to execution without bounding it. (b) "From the commit … the live store, its three Kenyan and one R3 replica: unreadable" is false on CockroachDB. The deleted row holding the wrapped subject key stays readable by `AS OF SYSTEM TIME` until `gc.ttlseconds`, and stays physically in Raft logs and in not-yet-compacted SSTables on every replica, R3's included. Anyone who can call the HSM under `KEK_PD,g` (identity's own workload, or break-glass plus the key service) can recover the subject key until generation g is destroyed. The real bound in **every** copy, the live one included, is generation destruction. (c) Live copies *before* the commit (the same subject key wrapped under g−1 in backups that are still retained) are not analysed. | Restate the bound from the request: **request → execution ≤ ‹erasure_exec_target› 10 days** (alert at 7, incident at 20); **‹kek_generation_period› 7 days**; **‹pd_backup_retention› 7 days**. Worst case: 10 + 7 + 7 = **24 days ≤ 30**. g−1 copies are gone by e + 7, and g copies (live MVCC and disk included) by e + 14. Set `gc.ttlseconds` on the store's key and item tables to at most 1 h, so `AS OF SYSTEM TIME` cannot reach erased keys. Rewrite the copy table: every copy, the live one included, becomes unreadable at the destruction of its generation, and `get` fails from the commit. Make generation destruction a scheduled, dual-authorised HSM quorum operation, not a physical ceremony (Decision D-2). Update C12 ("within `erasure_max` of the request"), RK-13, Benefits and DT-15. DT-15 must also measure from the request and cover `AS OF SYSTEM TIME` and a restored backup. |
| R5-02 | Blocker | §3.2, §3.7, §7.2 (erasure row), §7.5, §4.2, Traceability, header "Satisfies" | **X1-S39 and the v1.7 erasure request path are not designed.** (a) POL-19, POL-32 (both administrator rows) and X1-N11 say the data subject asks *through an administrator of its account* or *by a documented request to support*. The RFC has no administrator command that passes an erasure request on, and §4.2 has no row for it. (b) The documented-request intake of §7.5 records a request only if C2 `decide` permits the requested operation for the requester. No buyer principal is permitted to erase, so an erasure documented request is always refused at intake, and X1-S39's second row cannot happen. (c) §7.2 allows the reason "legal requirement", so one compliance operator can erase *any* subject's data, an operator's or an investigation target's included, without any request. v1.7 allows erasure only "at its request". The insider risk is real, because erasure is single-operator and irreversible. (d) The request time, which starts the `erasure_max` clock, is never recorded. (e) X1-S39 is absent from Traceability and "Satisfies" ("all 38"). | Add an identity-and-access command **pass on an erasure request** (subject = a user of this account or supplier, the administrator itself included), administrator-only, recorded with the time. It is the start of the clock. Extend the intake: a principal may make a documented request to erase **its own** personal data (the data subject is the requester's `principal_id`), recorded on the console log (requirement fix RQ-R5-1). The erasure action must cite one of those request records (`request_ref`). The personal-data store refuses `erase` without a valid, unexecuted request for the same subject, read from identity and the console through a named query. Remove "legal requirement" (raised as RQ-R5-3). Operators' own erasure requests come through workforce administration (RQ-R5-6). Add X1-S39 to Satisfies, Traceability and a design test: request → executed by one compliance operator → every copy unreadable within 30 days. |

Generate next month's data keys ahead (for example from day 25), so a month boundary needs no HSM call. Add the boundary to RK-9. |
| R5-21 | Minor | §3.3 (Edge caches), §3.1 (Sizing) | The load of cache fills is not sized. Every active key at every edge node is re-read every 30 s: at 10⁵ active keys and 50 edge nodes, that is about 1.7×10⁵ follower reads/s (est.), on a path the design calls read-light. A simpler design meets the bound by construction: each edge holds the whole key and session table (10⁷ keys × about 100 B ≈ 1 GB, est.), applied from a compacted identity stream, and **refuses all keys** when its applied position is more than 30 s behind identity's heartbeat. That removes per-key fills and negative caching, and makes the bound one invariant. | Size the fills, or adopt the full-replica edge with a lag watchdog (it also helps R5-11 and R5-12). Either way, state the fill read rate in §3.1. |
| R5-22 | Minor | §3.7 item (4), DT-14, §5.4 (Key release policy) | The continuous personal-data scanner reads "log tables (after decryption, in the audit enclave)". That needs every owner kind's **payload** keys, but the release policy gives audit the audit-column key only. The scanner would be the one component that can decrypt everything. | Give the scanner its own identity in the release policy, sampling rows (not every row) with no outputs other than findings. Or move the check to the write path: the owner runtime refuses a payload whose typed schema has a free-text field. Prefer the second, and keep sampling as the backstop. |
| R5-23 | Minor | §7.1, §2.1 (Workforce administration), T-31 | The set of persons who may approve role grants is not defined (any person with a registered WebAuthn credential?). The **person identifier** that four eyes compares is minted by workforce administration, so a workforce insider can give one human two person identifiers in the same role and defeat four eyes. | Name the approver set (workforce approvers who hold no operator role). Bind person identifiers to the vetting record, out of scope but attested. Add the residual to T-31, with detection: an alert when two identities in the same role share a device or authenticator attestation. |
| R5-24 | Minor | §2.2 step 5, C1, §10 (edge context signing keys), §9.2 | **Over-engineering.** Every principal context is Ed25519-signed per request (125,000/s with polls) and verified at the owner, but the signature is **not kept** in the audit field set. It adds no non-repudiation, and the owner already knows the edge from its mTLS peer identity. | Either drop per-request signatures (mTLS peer identity plus the allowlist) or keep the signature digest in `audit.principal`. Prefer dropping them, and keep identity-signed key records (R5-13), which do add something. |
| R5-25 | Minor | §12 | STRIDE gaps beyond T-33 (R5-13): build and supply-chain compromise of the shared libraries (C1, C2, C13 are in every owner); malware on an operator's managed device (session riding between hardware touches); compromise of the **audit verifier**, which signs checkpoints over whatever it is fed; key-service or HSM-administrator insider; DoS of the key service by unwrap floods at takeover; DoS of identity by refused-attempt recording. | Add rows with mitigations: reproducible builds and signed artefacts; per-action touch (R5-07); a second, independent verifier that rebuilds checkpoints from sealed segments monthly; HSM quorum authentication for key export or destroy; the unwrap limits already set; aggregation of refused-attempt records. |
| R5-26 | Minor | §3.2, §4.2 | **No account recovery.** X1-S38 keeps one administrator in the record, but nothing covers an administrator who leaves or loses the WebAuthn authenticator. No principal of the account may then make administrator changes, and a documented request cannot help, because it needs a principal who may make the change. Recovery paths are a classic takeover route. | Decision D-7: recovery through onboarding re-verification (KYB-level identity proofing), which grants the administrator right as the *onboarding* principal, recorded, with a notice to every remaining user and a 24 h hold before the new administrator can issue keys. Raised as RQ-R5-4. |
| R5-27 | Minor | Benefits, Competition, RK-13 | **NABC.** Benefits states "every copy unreadable within 44 days", against `erasure_max`. Competition lists no alternatives for meeting a 30-day bound: no backups of the store; per-day expiry keys; shorter generations. RK-13 still says "legal confirms the bound", which v1.7 has fixed as *Assumed* at 30 days. | Update after R5-01, R5-02 and R5-04. Add the Competition rows "no backups of the personal-data store" (rejected: one operator error loses contact data with no recovery) and "per-subject backup exclusion lists on restore" (rejected: an insider restoring a backup bypasses it). |
| R5-28 | Minor | §8.3 (One tenant per endpoint), C8 | "The notifier … takes [the tenant] from the event's partition." For supplier notices built from the link schedule feed, the partition is a **link**, not a tenant, so the supplier must come from the catalogue's link → supplier map. As written, this is either wrong or relies on something unstated. | State the tenant derivation per feed: account partition → account; link partition → the link's supplier from the catalogue at the event's position. |

## Decisions taken

Business decisions taken by this review so that no finding waits on one. Each is a
default that the coordinator may revisit without changing any mechanism.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D-1 | Erasure is executed within **10 days** of the request (alert at 7, incident at 20). `kek_generation_period` is **7 days**, `pd_backup_retention` **7 days**. Worst case from request to every copy unreadable: **24 days**, inside `erasure_max` (30) | Leaves 6 days of margin for a missed weekly rotation, while keeping 7 days of backups for operator error |
| D-2 | **No off-HSM escrow of the personal-data KEK.** Generations live only in the Kenyan HSM clusters and are destroyed by a scheduled HSM quorum operation (two of the named security officers authorise it electronically), not a vault ceremony. The log KEKs keep 3-of-5 escrow | Escrow is a copy that erasure must reach. A weekly vault ceremony would not be done reliably. Losing personal data in a four-cluster HSM loss costs no record or money |
| D-3 | Erasure needs a recorded **request**: an administrator's *pass on* command, or the subject's own documented request. There is no erasure "for legal requirement" without one (raised as RQ-R5-3) | v1.7 text. A single-operator irreversible action needs evidence that the owner can check |
| D-4 | A non-conforming payload falls under an erasure of any data subject of the account it was attributed to, or of the supplier whose exporter sent it, if it was received before the request. Over-erasing loses only dispute evidence that is already reduced to a digest | Payloads have no known subject. Missing an erasure is a legal breach; over-erasing is not |
| D-5 | Removing a user or closing an account does **not** erase by itself. The administrator passes the request on separately (one more step in the portal) | Keeps erasure a deliberate, recorded request, as POL-19 frames it |
| D-6 | **Every credential is revoked within 60 s**: agent and supplier keys, user sessions, administrator rights, and exporter, probe and network-operations certificates, with established connections closed | One bound to state, test and explain. A removed employee of a buyer should not keep booking for 5 min |
| D-7 | Account recovery is an **onboarding** action after re-verification, with a notice to the account's users and a 24 h hold before key issuance | Recovery is the usual takeover path. The hold gives the real administrator time to object |
| D-8 | Operator reads use one hardware touch per **case** (‹read_grant_ttl› 1 h, scoped to one target), not one per page | Keeps support usable while making every read attributable and owner-recorded |
| D-9 | Webhook endpoints: only port 443, with a generic registration error | Removes blind scanning through Share. No buyer need for other ports is known |

## Requirements issues (against `requirements-v1.7`)

| # | Issue | Proposal |
|---|---|---|
| RQ-R5-1 | **A documented request cannot ask for erasure.** POL-32 and the glossary define a documented request as one by "an authenticated principal of the account who may make that change itself". No buyer principal may erase (compliance only), so X1-S39's second row ("by a documented request to support") contradicts the definition, and POL-19 sends it "to support" although compliance executes it | Amend POL-32: "… or, for erasure, a request by the data subject's own principal concerning its own personal data", routed to compliance |
| RQ-R5-2 | **"An erasure it falls under" is undefined** for non-conforming payloads (POL-19, RQ3-14): a payload has no known data subject. Nor does it say whether "made unrecoverable … `dispute_window` + 7 days after receipt" covers backups | Adopt D-4, and state that the expiry covers every copy |
| RQ-R5-3 | **No path for erasure the law requires without a request** (a court order, or the end of a retention period for KYB documents of a closed account). v1.7 allows erasure only at the subject's request, so personal data of closed accounts is kept indefinitely, which may conflict with storage limitation under Kenya's DPA 2019 | Add: compliance erases without a request only under a recorded legal order (four eyes, like disclosure), and personal data has a retention per class after account closure (legal to set, POL-24) |
| RQ-R5-4 | **Account recovery has no owner** (POL-32 says naming every principal's actions "leaves no action without an owner") | Adopt D-7 as an onboarding action in POL-32 |
| RQ-R5-5 | **X1-N3's 60 s bound covers agent keys only.** User sessions, administrator-right removals and device certificates have no bound | Adopt D-6, in X1-N3 or POL-25 |
| RQ-R5-6 | **Operators' own erasure** has no request path: the path "through an administrator of its account" does not fit Share staff | Operators ask through workforce administration, and compliance executes, as for any subject |
| RQ-R5-7 | **X1-S15 "the attempt is refused"** cannot hold against a database or storage administrator with break-glass access, who can physically change bytes. What can hold is that it is detected, recorded and made unreadable | Reword: "refused, or, if made below the application with administrative access, detected at the next verification and recorded; no altered record is accepted by any reader" |
