## Verdict

**ACCEPT WITH CHANGES.** 0 Blockers, 6 Majors, 12 Minors (18 findings), plus 7 joint items
(3 of them Major) for the RFC-001/RFC-002 reconciliation.

Review 17's Blocker is resolved. The capacity-level model is correct: I re-ran review 17's
cases (a) to (d), P1-S43, S46, S47, S65, S76, S79 and S80 through §3.1, §5.2, §5.4 and §5.5, and
each gives the result the spec states. All 11 Majors of review 17 are resolved on RFC-001's
side. R1-04, R1-07 and R1-09 still fail end to end because RFC-002 has not been updated yet
(JI-1, JI-2). The monotonicity argument behind "the affected set only shrinks after the
projection settles" (§5.4) holds: I checked it against parts that end, parts that are
decreased, increases, and new sales, which must fit `K_s ≤ A_s ≤ L_R`.

What is left is around the edges of the new model, mostly where a reduction's cut meets
something else:

1. **The decided-but-unapplied cut in `occ_commit` has no complete lifecycle.** No rule says
   which command withdraws it, or how. Several sequences leave the committed rate understated,
   and the next reduction or outage is then decided on a number that is too low (R26-01).
2. **Outage flags during a reduction's notice** compare the uncut committed rate with the
   reduced announced level. Every holder is then flagged at minutes after `T_eff` that the
   outage does not overcommit (R26-02).
3. **Maintenance is counted as committed** in outage flags and in a reduction's excess. That
   contradicts the glossary's *Committed rate*, and it cuts a buyer permanently because of a
   supplier's temporary booking (R26-03).
4. **The single cut rate knowingly over-cuts** (R26-04). A successor **cannot recover `C_q`**
   after a takeover in the middle of the steps (R26-05). The reduction projection has **no
   cost or memory bound** on a hot link (R26-06).

## Decisions taken

| # | Decision | Why (buyers incl. AI agents, suppliers, Share's risk, operability) |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Maintenance never makes a buyer affected or cut.** It is outside the committed rate (as the glossary already says). At a reduction's `T_eff`, maintenance overlapping `[T_eff, ∞)` is trimmed to fit beside the surviving parts within the announced level, before any part's cut is final. During an outage it flags no reservation | The guarantee is the product. A supplier's temporary booking should not cost a buyer Mbps for ever, and the supplier "bears the consequences of its own works" (POL-07 *Why*). Agents can rely on "affected" meaning a real overcommitment. The supplier rebooks if it still needs the works |
| D2 | **From `T_eff`, the committed rate at a minute already reflects the reduction's cuts**, projected until they are decided. Outage flags use it | A cut is effective at `T_eff` (RFC-000 §4.5). Flagging minutes that only the reduction overcommits sends false notices and credits Share's money for no outage |
| D3 | **A cut target is a step function of the minute**, `[(from, r)…] \| none` | It is exact, RFC-002 already composes per minute, and it is cheaper than per-part targets. Buyers lose no more than POL-07 requires |
| D4 | **An equivalent-path move is final only once every leg of the new path is booked**. Only then are the old legs ended | P1-S61 and P1-S52 hold. The old link cuts only the affected Mbps on a failed move |
| D5 | **An offer-part cut binds only the move**, not the reservation's current path | POL-07 removes only Mbps that do not fit where the reservation actually is |
