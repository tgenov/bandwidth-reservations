## Verdict

**REJECT** (revise; a fix verification of the findings below is enough, not a full
re-review). The review finds **2 Blockers, 10 Majors and 16 Minors** (28 findings).

Most of the contract holds up: the push and catch-up gate, the edge invariant, the
envelope's six checks, the single-use operator check, erasure intake and objection, and
the signed webhook. These are close to RFC-005, and most traces below pass. Two defects
must not reach code:

- **The operator signature check cannot be implemented safely.** It is the control behind
  RFC-005's T-9 guarantee ("a compromised console cannot make an action take effect that
  its operators did not sign"), and the contract never says where an owner or reader gets
  an operator credential's public key, or that the credential belongs to the named
  operator (R46-01).
- **The restore role's row-level-security policy, as written, confines nothing.** It lets
  the break-glass restore role write every partition of a kind as soon as any one partition
  of that kind has an open stop (R46-02).

The Majors cluster in four places:

- Fencing of signed identity state across lease epochs: heartbeats from a deposed holder,
  and signing-key certificates with no revocation channel (R46-03, R46-04).
- Distribution channels the contract assumes but never defines: the policy matrix, and
  credential public keys (R46-05, with R46-01).
- The status-push consumer: `since` and the dedupe key (R46-07, R46-08).
- Operator-power edges: who may cancel, what a console session can read, the incomplete
  target union, audit-append forgery, and the stop that the owner runtime raises itself
  (R46-06, R46-09 … R46-12).

## Decisions taken

- **D1 (R46-01).** Identity verifies operator assertions inside `operator_check` before it
  consumes them, and returns the public key, so owners verify again. Identity already holds
  the keys, counters and consumed pairs. Owners verifying with identity's key cost nothing
  extra and leave forgery needing two compromised parties. This is the cheapest design that
  closes both forgery and counter-burning.
- **D2 (R46-06).** Only the requester (same `person`) may cancel, as today. The cancel
  carries the signed action, so identity can enforce it. I rejected letting any operator of
  the role cancel: it adds a sabotage path and no requirement asks for it.
- **D3 (R46-10).** S-G3 stands, with session-authority summaries made **purpose-bound**:
  mandatory `action_id` and `action_type`, the target kind matching the action, counted
  against `operator_read_limit`, breadth-alerted, and audited before answering.
  Exploratory reads still need a read grant. This keeps operators fast (one touch per case)
  while bounding what a stolen console identity can enumerate.
- **D4 (R46-24).** Webhook `occurred_at` stays a JSON integer. Epoch microseconds stay below
  2^53 until about year 2255, and buyers and AI agents parse integers more easily. The
  units rule gains an exception for the outbound webhook body. Keep RFC-005's single-notice
  `webhook-id` = event ID rule (it is compatible with S-M4, and lets single-notice
  receivers use either key).
- **D5 (R46-14).** Receivers enforce upper bounds on `not_after`. No signing-time field is
  added: the bound relative to the receiver's own clock is enough.

## Requirements issues

- **RQ46-1 (Minor).** X1-S11 ("Share sends a webhook to a **buyer**") and X1-S24
  ("**account's** endpoint") cover buyers only, but RFC-005 §8.3 and RFC-006 §15 deliver
  signed webhooks to **supplier** endpoints too (maintenance cut notices, P1-N8). No
  scenario requires a supplier to be able to verify a webhook, or that a supplier's webhook
  carries nothing about another supplier. Proposed amendment: add a supplier example to
  X1-S11 and X1-S24.

## Architecture issues

- **AR46-1 (Minor).** RFC-000 §8 "Signed webhook" lists only "Buyers" as consumers.
  Proposed amendment: "Buyers and suppliers".
- **AR46-2 (Major, with R46-04 and R46-05).** RFC-000 §8 has no row for distributing the
  **policy** (matrix, catalogue, reason codes, allowlist, read types) or the **signing-key
  certifications and their revocations**. Every owner, the edge and the console's signing
  client depend on both. Proposed amendment: add a row "Policy and key-certification feed:
  identity's policy partition and the key service → edge, every owner, readers and the
  console signing client; versioned, signed, applied only at higher versions; a
  certification revocation refused everywhere within `edge_staleness_max`" (owner RFC-005).
