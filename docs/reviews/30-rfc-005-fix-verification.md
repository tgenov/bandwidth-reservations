## Verdict

**ACCEPT WITH CHANGES.** No regression of R5-01 … R5-28, V-1 … V-4 or W-1 … W-3. The
rebase meets X1-S44, X1-S45, P3-S71 and P3-S75 against v1.9, and AI4-11 against RFC-000
rev 4.3. Five defects remain, all in the new contract surface (Y-1 … Y-5). Each fix is a
precise local edit of a few lines, so the orchestrator may apply them directly without
another round.

## Rebase items (v1.9, RFC-000 rev 4.3)

| RQ-R5-8, X1-S44 | Verified | §3.2 held list now covers grantees transitively (grant entry names `recovery_ref`). §3.8 `recovery_cancelled` removes every held right in one entry, refuses held pass-ons with the subject told, and keeps `added_in_hold` users named in the notice. `recovery_confirmed` makes held rights ordinary (amender decision 5) and opens held requests with `requested_at` unchanged (decision 4). The rows trace as follows. Row 1: u3 held, its key refused, nothing else changes. Row 2: one entry removes both rights. Row 3: u4 kept and named. Row 4: *held* until 10:00, then *open*, and the clock runs from 11:00. Row 5: refused, u5 told. Bound: `e` ≤ `r` + 10 still holds, because the hold uses at most 1 of the 10 days, so the worst case stays at 24 days. A *held* request cannot be executed, because §3.7 Execution accepts only *open* or *to verify*. Crash case: a late `recovery_confirmed` only delays the end of the restrictions, and the objection race is first-commit-wins as before. Consistent with POL-32 v1.9 text, T-43, DT-26, DT-29 |

| P3-S71, P3-S75, RQ3-17 | Verified, **defect Y-3** | The record keeps the identity, the keyed digest and the reason, and nothing more. ## Defects

| # | Item | Location | Failure | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Y-1 | Set-scoped probe credit (RFC-004 rev 5 interface) | §7.3 "Sending" bullet; §7.4 check 6; §4.4 target-summaries row; RFC-004 §B12 (interface point) | (a) RFC-004 §B12 retries a member refused `kyb_not_vetted` "under the same envelope … with a fresh `action_id` derived from the action and the attempt". Under this RFC that cannot verify. The digest covers `action_id` (§7.3, C15: "a signature cannot be re-sent under a new identifier"), so check 1 or 2 fails. And the assertion is already consumed under the original `action_id`, so check 3 answers `unused` = false. The retry path the two RFCs claim to share does not exist. (b) Check 6 says "`target_version` is still current in its own state". Applied to a set, that refuses every remaining member once placement moves past v during the fan-out, and no account owner holds the slot map's version anyway. Check 6 also says "a member of the set at version v", while RFC-004 F5 checks the account's own `excluded` flag (F9). (c) The signing client must show an owner-signed summary of the target (§7.3), but no owner is named as signer for a set descriptor (placement is absent from §4.4's summaries row) | (a) Add to §7.3 "Sending": "A member that refuses is not retried under this action; finance signs a new set-scoped action for the members still without the limit." Record an interface point asking RFC-004 §B12 to drop the "fresh `action_id`" retry. (b) Check 6, set-scoped clause: "instead of `target_version`, `member` equals the owner's partition and the account is designated a probe account in the owner's own state (RFC-004 F9 `excluded`)". (c) Add placement (slot map at v: count of designated accounts) to §4.4's target-summaries row, or state that a set descriptor's summary is the descriptor itself, membership being checked by each owner |
| Y-2 | RQ4-14, C16 | C16 Query; §3.2 supplier row | `payout_destination(supplier, at)` is "a consistent read by identity at `at`". RFC-004 §B7.1 retries on "temporarily unavailable" (possibly hours after the cut), and a re-instruction "reuses" the reference and version but needs the details again. A time-travel read fails beyond the MVCC window: the `pds` tables have `gc.ttlseconds` ≤ 1 h. Nothing says that a superseded version's details item survives a later change. An implementer is then pushed toward reading the *current* destination, which defeats "applies from the next payout run" | C16: "identity resolves the version in force at `at` from the destination's version history (each version with its commit timestamp, in identity's own log or projection), not by `AS OF SYSTEM TIME`. Each version's details are a separate store item keyed by (`destination_ref`, version), kept while any instruction may reference it. A re-instruction reads by (`destination_ref`, version)." Add "payout destination" to the purposes of identity's `get` (§3.7 table) |
| Y-3 | Store events feed (withdrawals) | §3.7 "Generations and destruction", last sentence; C12 "Store events feed"; `withdraw_payload` row | Identity "publishes" `payload_withdrawn`, but the withdrawal is a transaction in `pds`, which has no changefeed and no outbox. No step writes the event atomically with, or before, the answer "withdrawn". Failure case: the store crashes after the delete commits and before the event is published. Ingestion has its answer and stops retrying. A later restore of a store backup taken before the withdrawal then resurrects the payload, because the feed has no withdrawal to re-apply. The payload stays readable until its day's expiry, against P3-S75's "kept nowhere" | `withdraw_payload` row and C12: "the store answers *withdrawn* only after identity has committed the `payload_withdrawn` entry to its outbox (the feed's source). A crash before that leaves the call unanswered, and ingestion's retry (idempotent) re-drives it." State the same for `generation_destroyed`: published from the attestation entry identity commits, before the generation counts as destroyed |
| Y-4 | `integrity_resume` (AI-R5-7, X1-S15) | §9.3 "Stop and restore after a detection", last sentence; C5 "Integrity stop"; DT-31 | "Rows after the last verified point that cannot be recovered are an incident …, never re-created" still lets audit send `integrity_resume` with the partition's head behind the first-seen head. The owner would then append new rows at sequences that consumers and audit have already seen with other content (a fork). Genuine committed decisions would be silently lost, for example an identity key revocation, which un-revokes the key at the account owner's next catch-up, or a ledger movement already on the feed | §9.3 and C5: "`integrity_resume` is sent only if the restored partition's chain re-verifies end to end **and its head equals audit's first-seen head**. Otherwise the partition stays stopped, and any resume with lost rows is a recorded incident decision (compliance and platform security), never automatic." Add to DT-31: "a restore that cannot reach the first-seen head leaves the partition stopped" |
| Y-5 | Backup key fallback (R5-11 regression risk) | §10 backup key row (fallback); T-19; RK-15 | The fallback puts `encryption_passphrase` "in the job record replicated to R3", which the row calls a "T-19 residual". But T-19 and RK-15 still claim "no secrets in system ranges" / "secret-free system ranges" (R5-11's fix), and neither lists it. The R3 operator would hold the key to every backup (column-sealed contents, and the store's wrapped keys) | If the fallback is kept, append to T-19's residual and RK-15: "under the passphrase fallback only (§10), the backup passphrase in job records; backups stay in R1 and R2, and their contents are column-sealed or `KEK_PD`-wrapped". Otherwise drop the fallback and let backups wait for EKM (§14 row) |

Nits, not defects:

- §3.7 RD-1 says "payloads average far above 1 KB (RFC-006 plans 100 KB)". That figure
  is above `payload_max_bytes` 64 KB, so under these caps no 100 KB payload is ever kept.
  Say that RFC-006's figure is a conservative bound.
- §7.3 out-of-band confirmation: for a set-scoped action, send one confirmation for the
  action, not one per member (about 16,384).
- RFC-003 counts drops from `ingest_log`, while §3.7 says "the store counts its cap
  refusals and withdrawals". Name one source of the reported counts to avoid double
  counting.

All five fixes are local and touch at most a few lines in each named place. None affects
another finding or an earlier fix. Y-1 (a) also needs one line in RFC-004 §B12 (interface
point), and Y-2 aligns RFC-004 §B7.1's "reuses them" without changing it.
