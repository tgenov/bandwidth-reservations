## Verdict

**REJECT** (draft.1). There is 1 Blocker, 7 Majors and 12 Minors.

The core safety mechanisms hold when traced through crash points:
- arrival-order hold decisions with tombstone-first checks;
- request-scoped release that ends every attempt;
- attempts numbered only by the link;
- the displacement-marker race, ordered by the serialisable read;
- per-minute `min` composition of cut targets;
- the C4-1 reply-as-answer.

I found no path to **oversell** (P1-N1). Every lost or reordered message either lowers
occupancy late or never, and never raises it.

The Blocker is in a binding decision, C4-3. Its rationale ("because rates are absolute, a
lost, repeated or reordered change never leaves a leg at a rate the composition did not
give") is false. A leg change names only the parts it changes, and `end_leg` and
`reduce_parts` start at different minutes. So a newer change that overtakes an older one
makes the older one `STALE_CHANGE`, and its effect is **lost for good**. The account owner
has already reported the capacity freed (E12), but the link still counts it. This is stranded
capacity, and the reported facts contradict the link's state. The fix is small (see
Decisions D1). The rest:
- yield re-asks are swallowed by notification de-duplication (POL-02 liveness);
- `cut_target` cannot be routed to an account partition;
- P1-S84's move can race its own `reduce_parts`;
- offer holds can be stranded for up to `offer_window` (7 days);
- M-2's unavailable link can block E8 for ever;
- the errata list is incomplete;
- the displacement table's key is described two ways.

### Traces that hold (no finding)

- **P1-S78, three rows.** First, the reduction commits first: the placeholder intents are laid
  in its first statement, so a later E5 read meets the intent, times out after 10 ms, rolls
  back the savepoint and defers. The next batch sees the marker, so there is no E5, and the
  leg is handled as displaced. Second, E5 reads first: the timestamp cache pushes the
  reduction, which retries above the read. The convert then meets the mark, and the link
  flags the part and alerts. Third, `DISPLACED` arrives with no E5: a later E5 reads the
  marker. One decision per request holds in every row, because E5 and E7 are both deciding
  entries on the `decision` row.
- **Yield and displacement on one attempt.** `yield_accept(a)` and `displacement_accept(a)`
  are ordered in the link log. The second gets `STALE_ATTEMPT` (handling: m3). The marker
  goes with the first. `GRANTED(a+1)` may arrive before the `ACCEPTED` reply; it is recorded,
  and that is consistent.
- **Hold and release reordered by `Moving`**: the release tombstones first, then the hold
  gets `TOMBSTONED`. **Expiry**: the hold is kept past `D` and `expire_if_undecided` is
  re-sent until `convert`/`release`. The link never releases on its own (P2-S104, P2-S106).
- **Cut target revisions**: rev 2 is applied before rev 1, and rev 1 then gives
  `APPLIED([])`; the highest revision governs the cap. Per-minute `min` makes the result
  independent of order. The reply comes only after E11 commits, and is re-sent across a
  link takeover.

## Decisions taken

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | B1 is fixed by **one outstanding leg change per (reservation, leg)**, not by full-state leg changes | It is the smallest change that makes C4-3's rule safe. The link side is unchanged, and the wire form is unchanged. The cost is a few milliseconds of serialisation on one leg, which no buyer-visible path waits on except E12. A cut never waits on a buyer change for more than one acknowledgement |
| D2 | Yield claims get a link-assigned `claim` sequence (M1) | Preserves POL-02 for crossings that re-form, with no new state at the account owner. The re-ask is rare, so the cost is negligible |
| D3 | On a new leg, P1-S84's `reduce_parts` follows `ACCEPTED` (M3), rather than carrying the cut pieces inside `offer_accept` | It reuses D1's per-leg sequencing, and keeps one meaning for `offer_accept` (RFC-001 §5.6, net-unchanged move) |
| D4 | `offer_release` goes to every leg that was sent `offer_hold`, and `offer_hold` is refused `TOMBSTONED` (M4) | Suppliers lose at most minutes of sellable capacity instead of up to 7 days. It is the same pattern as the request-scoped release, so there is one mechanism to test |
| D5 | Settled M-2 stands: `LINK_UNAVAILABLE(link)` with no counter-offer. An unavailable link's answer to a release counts as the acknowledgement (M5) | Buyers, including AI agents, get a prompt, final and machine-readable rejection rather than a stuck `pending`. The funds hold is freed. Nothing can be held on a retired link, so Share takes no risk |
| D6 | Cut targets may be batched per destination account slot (m6) | It is cheap and operable for an 11–50 person team. It keeps C4-1's synchronous reply semantics per target |
