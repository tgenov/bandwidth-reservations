## Verdict

**REJECT: revise to revision 4.** 1 Blocker, 11 Majors, 13 Minors (25 findings).

The core holds up. The pulse/impulse model and the sparse monoid tree are right, and every
P1 worked example I re-ran gives the result the spec states (P1-S01, S12, S16, S38, S47, S50,
S65, S70, S72, S73, S74, S77). The ordering argument for the displacement marker is sound in
CockroachDB's serialisable model. Three things fail:

1. **Capacity arithmetic.** Every capacity fact is stored as a delta from a "current"
   capacity that already includes temporary outages. If facts overlap, this oversells,
   revokes permanently because of an outage, and refuses valid supplier commands (R1-01).
2. **The substrate behaves differently from what the RFC assumes.** Batches have a fixed
   commit timestamp, so any read at the present timestamp of a range a batch is about to
   write forces a retry. The RFC's own standby tail does this to every batch on the hottest
   link (R1-02). Displacement-marker reads do it to a reduction's batch on a busy link
   (R1-03). A paused deposed owner can block the takeover read (R1-10). The outbox identity
   does not fit RFC-006's consumer rule (R1-08).
3. **Joint contracts disagree with RFC-002** on `wait`, attempts, revocations and moves
   (R1-04, R1-06, R1-07, R1-09). Each gap lets a scenario fail in code even though both
   RFCs look right when read on their own.

| R1-01 | **Blocker** | §3.1, §5.2, §5.4, §5.5, §5.7, §11.1 | **Capacity facts are deltas from "current" capacities, and the deltas do not compose.** `record_outage`: Δ = current physical − `to_capacity`. `announce_reduction`: Δ = current *sellable* − `new_capacity`. `record_increase`: Δ = `new_capacity` − current sellable. Sellable capacity includes open outages (§3.1 table), and the reduction greedy's excess is `occ_phys − C_ref`, which includes open outages. Worked on P1's background (link 10, A and B 4 Mbps open-ended each): **(a)** An outage to 6 is open (Δ 4) and the supplier announces a permanent reduction to 4, so Δ = 6 − 4 = 2. The outage is restored before `T_eff` and the impulse −4 is removed. At `T_eff` the excess is 8 + 2 − 10 = 0, so B is never cut. The link then carries 8 Mbps of reservations on 4 Mbps. That is a **silent oversell**: no outage is open, so nothing is flagged. **(b)** During the same outage, an announcement to 8 gives Δ = 6 − 8 < 0, and a valid supplier command is refused `INVALID`. **(c)** A reduction to 6 (Δ 4) and an outage to 9 (Δ 1) still open at `T_eff` give an excess of 8 + 4 + 1 − 10 = 3, so B is cut by 3 instead of 2. That is a **permanent cut caused by an unplanned outage**, which POL-07 forbids ("confirmed reservations are not revoked"; "never more than the reduced capacity then requires"). **(d)** An outage to 6 (Δ 4) and then an increase to 15 give Δ = 15 − 6 = 9. Sellable capacity becomes 15 while the link carries 6, and 19 after the restoration. The Benefits claim "never oversold" does not hold | Model capacity facts as **levels**. Keep a nominal sellable level `N_s(t)` (registration, announcements from `floor_minute(ts)`, increases from `ceil_minute(ts)`) and a nominal physical level `N_p(t)` (announcements from `T_eff`), each as one timeline. Treat each open outage as a cap `L_i` over its range. Effective sellable is `min(N_s, L_i…)` and effective physical is `min(N_p, L_i…)`. Every fact recomputes the effective step function over the range it touches and adds the *difference* as impulses against `C_ref`, so the trees and queries do not change. The reduction greedy uses `N_p` and committed parts only, never the outage caps. `INVALID` checks compare against nominal levels. Add T-21: every pair of overlapping capacity facts, cross-checked against a level oracle. See Decisions D1 and RQ-17-1 |

## Decisions taken

| # | Decision | Why (buyers incl. AI agents, suppliers, Share's risk, operability) |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | **An outage's `to_capacity` is an absolute level**, the capacity the link carries while the outage lasts. A permanent increase recorded during an outage raises the nominal capacity but not the effective capacity until the restoration. A permanent reduction may be announced during an outage, to any level below the nominal sellable capacity | Buyers are never sold Mbps the link cannot carry. Suppliers can record what is physically true without fighting the arithmetic. Share's SLA exposure follows the physical facts. This replaces RFC-001 §5.5's "an increase … raises the capacity the outage is measured from" |
| D2 | **`wait = false` is decided at the link**, from a `wait` flag in the hold | The link's check is the one-at-a-time check of POL-05. Its refusal's tombstone is the release acknowledgement, which saves a commit. No younger holder yields for a request that will not wait |
| D3 | **"Recorded at" is the commit timestamp, exactly.** P1-S76's "09:31:00" means a record whose timestamp is exactly the boundary. A record at 09:31:00.3 takes effect from 09:32 | This is the only reading that is checkable from the records. T-20 builds the boundary case by controlling the timestamp |
| D4 | **A permanent-reduction cut is a per-link target rate**, composed by `min` across links at the account owner | A route has one rate. It gives "just enough" across several reductions and idempotency by (link, reduction, reservation) |
| D5 | **Rating's closure may wait for a reduction's final step** for reservations in its affected set (bounded by the step time, minutes) | It keeps POL-07's "decided at the notice end" and RFC-000's exact closure, at the cost of a short rating delay on a rare supplier exit |
