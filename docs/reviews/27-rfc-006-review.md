## Verdict

**REJECT: revise to revision 5.** The review finds 3 Blockers, 11 Majors and 13 Minors (27
findings).

Revision 4 is a real improvement. Review 20's three Blockers are fixed as proposed:
revocation on acquisition, read leases inside the lease with the post-acquisition wait, and
sharded control rows. The capacity, archive, Kafka `min.insync.replicas` and measurement
findings are also resolved. The rejection comes from three new mechanisms that revision 4
added without carrying the reporting and closure proofs over to them:

1. **Resuming a lapsed session** (R6-03's fix) gives a holder its slots back without the
   acquisition rules. A reader can then be leased below an entry that rule 3 has already
   answered, and answer `pending` afterwards (R27-01).
2. **Partition moves** (R6-09's fix) give no reporting fence between the old and the new
   slot. A reader leased by the old slot's holder can answer `pending` after the new slot
   has reported (R27-02).
3. **The relay's resolved and `CLOSED` markers** are not sound with how CockroachDB
   partitions a changefeed into Kafka, or with a scaled-out stateless relay. The
   effective-time closure that rating relies on can be published early (R27-03).

Each fix is local and stated below. All three interleavings belong in the TLA+ model and the
Jepsen harness (§22.2) before any owner RFC is approved against §12.

## Decisions taken

None of these blocks the RFC. Each weighs buyers (including AI agents), suppliers, Share's
risk and operability.

| # | Question | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Metro split: consolidate only, or also let owners reach leaseholders through the other region's gateways? | **Both.** The R2 gateway fallback is always on; consolidation and drain follow (R27-04) | About 10 ms more per commit in a rare state is far cheaper than an outage of half the slots. Buyers keep service |
| D2 | Kafka failure detection: 9 to 10 s, or about 3 to 4 s with more ISR churn? | **About 3 to 4 s** (R27-06) | Feeds carry 10 s targets (X2-S22, X2-S17). Facts are in the database, so an ISR flap costs nothing durable |
| D3 | Anycast when both regions' canaries fail | **Never withdraw the last announcing region.** Serve errors rather than black-hole (R27-07) | Clients, especially AI agents, retry on errors. A withdrawn prefix needs BGP re-convergence, which takes minutes |
| D4 | First attempts to a failing endpoint | **Fixed reserved concurrency of 16 and a 1 to 2 s connect timeout for first attempts.** AIMD only for retries (R27-08) | POL-07 and POL-04 "told" are owed to the buyer whatever their endpoint's state. The extra load on a dead endpoint is bounded |
| D5 | Staffing model | **One primary platform rotation of at least 6** (database, Kafka, runtime, network first response), network and carriers as secondary. **About 12 rostered engineers is a go-live condition** (R27-14) | A small company cannot staff two six-person rotations. Vendor 24 × 7 support (database, Kafka, cloud) carries the depth |
| D6 | Hot-link moves at load | **Allowed, with pre-warm**, bounded `Moving` (R27-09) | The trigger is load. Waiting for "low load" would leave the hottest link on a saturated shared slot |
| D7 | Account slot-count changes | **Double only, moving existing accounts lazily, probe accounts last** (R27-10) | It avoids a platform-wide re-route, and keeps every slot probed throughout |

## Requirements issues

| # | Issue | Where | Proposal |
|---|---|---|---|
| RQ-R6-3 | **The slot rule turns the latency tail into unavailability.** With the 200 ms per-probe limit and about 16,384 slots, a 0.1% share of probes slower than 200 ms (an edge GC pause, the pending timer's thin slack) costs about 0.7 of the 4.32 minutes a month in each (capability, region), and 0.2% costs about 5.7, the whole budget. The slot rule (RQ-R6-1) was meant to catch a **stuck slot**, which latency targets cannot see. Latency is already governed by the p99 targets | POL-06 (available minute, slot rule), X2-S30, X2-S18 | For rule 3 only, count a probe as failing if it got no correct answer within a looser bound (for example 1 s, or `ack_budget` + 300 ms). Keep the 200 ms limit for the 90% rule and the latency targets. A stuck slot is still caught at its first minute |
