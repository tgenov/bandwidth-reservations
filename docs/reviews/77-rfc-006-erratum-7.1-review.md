Reviewer's model. To test the rule set with numbers, I wrote a discrete-event model of one
slot lane with the rules of 5a, 5b and 5c exactly as written (entry > 500 ms at 2 checks of
100 ms, or an abandon at 2,000 ms; release ≤ 250 ms for 1 s and 1 s after the last trigger;
nearest-rank p99 over 2 s; `t_entry` weight 0.3 over batches of ≥ 16 entries, `max` after
an abandon; `n_cap` floor 16; `retryAfterMs` = clamp(`q · t_entry` + 400, 250, 5,000) ×
U[0.8, 1.2]; no fresh attempt after an abandon; callers re-send at once after `Retry` and
after `retryAfterMs` after `Overloaded`, as in the test). Database time is the test's model,
`d` = 50 ms + 2 ms per row, unless stated. The model is the reviewer's, not code; the figures
below are estimates from it.

## Verdict

**REJECT** (erratum 7.1 only; revision 7's acceptance is not in question).

2 Blocker, 6 Major, 12 Minor.

The diagnosis (steps i to iv) is correct, and the direction (admission on time, a time-budgeted
batch, no blind fresh attempt) is right. The safety arguments of §4.2 and §4.3 hold: 5b takes a
per-partition prefix, 5c rolls back before `COMMIT`, and the commit fence and the POL-26 deadline
conditions run inside the transaction, so a late re-proposal can only fail its condition. But
the rule set as specified does not give recovery. (1) Its release limit (250 ms) is below the
fixed point of its own batch sizing (400 ms). So a slot that drains a backlog at full rate cannot
leave the overloaded state. It must go idle for about 3 s, and then it admits a synchronised herd.
In the test model this is a limit cycle for the whole 30 s, with zero-goodput seconds every
about 7 s. (2) 5b collapses goodput by about 10 times when the database's slowness is a fixed
cost per transaction (slow sync, far leaseholder). §22.1 run (b) injects exactly this. The
§22.1 exit criteria cannot be met as written, and DT-50 and the existing test pass an
oscillating slot.

## Checks that passed

- **Safety of §4.2 and §4.3.** 5b takes a queue prefix, which is a per-partition prefix, so
  one transaction in flight per partition, contiguous `seq` and monotone `ts` hold. 5c rolls
  back before `COMMIT`, so nothing ambiguous arises. The fence, the conditions, `decide` at
  `ctx.ts`, the head upserts with `batch_id` and the `Ambiguous` gate are unchanged.
  Dropping the fresh attempt removes no guarantee that an owner relies on (R1-1 depends on
  restarts, not on the fresh attempt). A re-proposal that arrives after the POL-26 fence fails
  its in-transaction condition, so there is no oversell and no hold is stranded. Only liveness
  changes.
- **Lease timing.** 5a and 5b do no database work and do not touch the session, the 2.9 s
  holder view, `leaseValid` or the renewal, which is not shed. Resolution reads and
  marker-only transactions are not shed.
- **CI6-6** is correctly scoped. C01 gives no owner a guarantee about which calls share a
  batch. The time cap is a prefix cap like the hard caps.
- **RFC-000.** No amendment is needed. RFC-000 says only that the runtime abandons.
- **Arithmetic checked and correct:** 500/70 ≈ 7; 2,000/500 = 4; 400/30 ≈ 13; 2,000/5 = 400;
  256 × 10 checks/s = 2,560; 23,600 × 100 B ≈ 2.4 MB; `n_cap`' = `n`/5 after a 2,000 ms
  abandon.

Share's risk is a few failed requests during an overload, not a collapse. |
